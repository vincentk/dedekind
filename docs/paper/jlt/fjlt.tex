\section{Featherweight Jlt}
\label{app:fjlt}
% Design (decided on #981): no overlapping instances; fold and explicit fuel as
% the only recursion; laws as ordinary values; multi-parameter instances with
% contexts; associated types and same-type constraints as in F^G; instance
% resolution terminates because contexts mention only proper subterms;
% struct templates are type operators (abbreviations); dictionaries are
% resolved at concrete types, as C++ instantiates them.

\subsection{Syntax}
\label{app:fjlt:syntax}

FJlt is a subset of \Cpp{23}.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-syntax} gives its grammar in the style of Featherweight Java: $\overline{\alpha}$ abbreviates a sequence $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$, a construct written over a sequence is repeated for each element, and the separators the host language wants (commas, \cppinline{&&}, semicolons) are implied.
Every FJlt program is a \Cpp{23} program with the same meaning; the converse fails by design.
The listings of Section~\ref{sec:translation} are FJlt programs, with \cppinline{static_assert(e)} read as the claim that the closed term $e$ evaluates to \cppinline{true}.

Metavariables: $\alpha, \beta$ range over type variables, $b$ over base types (\cppinline{bool}, \cppinline{size_t}), $O$ over the function-object types, the standard templates (\cppinline{std::plus}, \cppinline{std::logical_and}, \ldots) and the operations a program declares, $r$ over ranges, $\oplus$ over the \emph{total} operations of the base types: on unsigned carriers addition, subtraction, multiplication and the bitwise operations, which wrap, and the comparisons; on \cppinline{bool} the logical operations; division and remainder are partial and not admitted, and the standard function objects $O$ are those of the same operations, $C$ over concept names, the standard ones included (\cppinline{std::semiregular} is the shape Rule~1 requires of every carrier), $S$ over the two standard result-type concepts \cppinline{std::same_as} and \cppinline{std::convertible_to}, $f$ over function names, $x$ over term variables and $c$ over constants.

\cppinline{std::ranges::fold_left} is the one recursion primitive, over a range whose length is fixed before the fold starts: \cppinline{repeat(x, n)}, $n$ copies of $x$, or \cppinline{iota(0, n)}, the first $n$ naturals.}

\subsection{Typing}
\label{app:fjlt:typing}

Three judgments, in the usual shape.
$\Delta \Vdash Q$ says that the constraint $Q$ is \emph{entailed} in the context $\Delta$, which holds type variables and assumed constraints.
$\Delta; \Gamma \vdash e : \tau$ types a term.
$\vdash D$ says a declaration is well formed, and a program is well formed when its declarations are and its closed term has a type.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} gives the rules that carry weight; the ones for constants, pairs, optionals, conditionals and the arithmetic of base types are the obvious ones and are omitted.

\paragraph{Resolution.}
A trait call $f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}$ at concrete types \emph{resolves} to a declaration of $f$: the explicit specialisation at $\overline{\tau}$ if there is one, otherwise the generic function whose head matches $\overline{\tau}$ and whose context is entailed, recursively.
The deleted primary never resolves, which is how an operation nobody registered fails a shape.
Well-formedness requires resolution to be a partial function: no two specialisations at one head, and no two generic functions whose heads both match an instantiation (the no-overlap condition); and it requires every context to mention only proper parts of the head's type arguments, so that resolution terminates~\cite{sulzmann2007understanding}.
It also orders the declarations: the body of an instance, a generic function or an operation may call only declarations that precede it, and a trait call in a body may resolve only to a declaration that precedes the body.
So no declaration calls itself, directly or through others, the translation needs no recursive binding, and the only recursion in a program is \cppinline{fold_left}'s.

\qquad

\\[12pt]

\\[12pt]
\textsc{(T-Fold)}\quad \dfrac{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash r : \texttt{range<}\tau\texttt{>} \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash a : \sigma \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash f : \sigma \times \tau \to \sigma}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash \texttt{fold\_left(}r\texttt{, }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} : \sigma}
\qquad

\\[12pt]

\\[12pt]
\textsc{(D-Instance)}\quad \dfrac{\varnothing;\; \overline{x : \tau_x} \vdash e : \tau}{\vdash \texttt{template <> constexpr } \tau\; f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\overline{\tau_x\; x}\texttt{) \{ return } e\texttt{; \}}}

\textsc{E-Law} is where a law is a value: entailment evaluates it, so a shape with a law is satisfied only at instances whose certificate holds, and the stratification of laws (closed terms of base type) keeps typing and evaluation from chasing each other.
\textsc{E-Op} reads $S$ as equality for \cppinline{std::same_as} and as conversion for \cppinline{std::convertible_to}.
\textsc{T-Trait-Assumed} is the rule that makes \textsc{D-Generic} a definition check: inside a constrained body a trait is reachable only through a constraint in $\Delta$.
\cppinline{iota(0, n)} is typed like \cppinline{repeat} with $\tau = $ \cppinline{size_t}.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{app:fjlt:evaluation}

Evaluation is small-step and call-by-value on closed terms, $e \longrightarrow e'$, with values and evaluation contexts
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
v &::=& c \mid f \mid O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{v}\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::pair\{}v\texttt{, }v\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::optional\{}v\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::nullopt} \\
E &::=& [\,] \mid f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{v}\texttt{, }E\texttt{, }\overline{e}\texttt{)} \mid E\texttt{(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \mid v\texttt{(}\overline{v}\texttt{, }E\texttt{, }\overline{e}\texttt{)} \mid E \oplus e \mid v \oplus E \mid E \texttt{ ? } e \texttt{ : } e \mid \ldots
\end{array}
\]
where the elided contexts are the remaining argument positions of pairs, optionals, projections, constructions and folds, each evaluated left to right.
It is \Cpp{}'s constant evaluation restricted to the grammar, and Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval} gives the rules; $e \Downarrow v$ abbreviates $e \longrightarrow^{*} v$.
The context grammar picks one redex per non-value term, so the relation is deterministic.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\footnotesize
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\textsc{(S-Call)} & f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{v}\texttt{)} \longrightarrow e[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \quad \text{where } f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>} \text{ resolves to a declaration with parameters } \overline{x} \text{ and body } e \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-Op)} & O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{v_y}\texttt{\}(}\overline{v}\texttt{)} \longrightarrow e[\overline{v_y}/\overline{y}][\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \quad \text{for a declared operation with body } e \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-Std)} & O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{\}(}\overline{c}\texttt{)} \longrightarrow c \quad \text{for a standard function object, } c \text{ the value the standard assigns} \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-Prim)} & c_1 \oplus c_2 \longrightarrow c \quad \text{the base type's operation, total by the choice of } \oplus \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-If)} & \texttt{true ? } e_1 \texttt{ : } e_2 \longrightarrow e_1 \qquad \texttt{false ? } e_1 \texttt{ : } e_2 \longrightarrow e_2 \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-Proj)} & \texttt{std::pair\{}v_1\texttt{, }v_2\texttt{\}.first} \longrightarrow v_1 \qquad O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{v}\texttt{\}.}y_i \longrightarrow v_i \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-Fold)} & \texttt{fold\_left(repeat(}v\texttt{, }k{+}1\texttt{), }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} \longrightarrow \texttt{fold\_left(repeat(}v\texttt{, }k\texttt{), }f\texttt{(}a\texttt{, }v\texttt{), }f\texttt{)} \\
 & \texttt{fold\_left(repeat(}v\texttt{, 0), }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} \longrightarrow a \\[3pt]
\textsc{(S-Ctx)} & E[e] \longrightarrow E[e'] \quad \text{if } e \longrightarrow e' \\
\end{array}
\]
\normalsize
\caption{Evaluation.
\textsc{S-Call} covers specialisations and generic functions alike, since resolution has already chosen the body; for a generic function the explicit type arguments are substituted first.
\textsc{S-Fold} over \cppinline{iota(i, j)} steps to \cppinline{iota(i + 1, j)} with \cppinline{f(a, i)} when $i < j$ and to $a$ otherwise.
Constants, pairs and optionals are values; \textsc{S-Prim} and \textsc{S-Std} are total because the admitted operations are, which is what Lemma~\ref{lem:progress} needs.}
\label{fig:fjlt-eval}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Translation into System F}
\label{app:fjlt:translation}

The target is System~F with products, sums and the base types of Section~\ref{sec:posture} taken as constants with their operations; records are nested pairs, and the base types could be Church-encoded, so the target is conservative over System~F.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-translation} gives the translation.
Its one non-local ingredient is the \emph{dictionary} of an entailment: $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket$ is the record that witnesses $Q$, built by the same recursion as resolution.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\scriptsize
\[
\begin{array}{l@{\quad}l}
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{types}} \\
\llbracket \alpha \rrbracket = \alpha \qquad \llbracket b \rrbracket = b & \\
\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \overline{\llbracket \tau_y \rrbracket} \times (\forall\overline{\beta}.\,\overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket) & \text{a function object: its fields and its call function} \\
\llbracket \texttt{std::pair<}\tau_1\texttt{, }\tau_2\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \llbracket \tau_1 \rrbracket \times \llbracket \tau_2 \rrbracket & \\
\llbracket \texttt{std::optional<}\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket = 1 + \llbracket \tau \rrbracket & \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{constraints, as record types}} \\
\llbracket C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \{\,\overline{r_i : \llbracket R_i \rrbracket[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}]}\,\} \text{ where } \texttt{concept } C \texttt{ = } \overline{R} & \text{one field per requirement} \\
\llbracket Q \rrbracket \text{ for a nested shape } Q & \text{its record type} \\
\llbracket e \rrbracket = \mathit{Bool} \text{ for a law } e & \\
\llbracket \texttt{\{ }e\texttt{ \} -> }S\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket & \text{an operation on parameters } \overline{x : \tau_x} \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{dictionaries, from entailment derivations}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket = q \text{ if } Q \in \Delta \text{ with record variable } q & \text{\textsc{E-Assume}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \{\,\overline{r_i = \llbracket \Delta \Vdash R_i[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}\,\} & \text{\textsc{E-Shape}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash e \rrbracket = \mathit{true} & \text{\textsc{E-Law}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash \texttt{\{ }e\texttt{ \} -> }S\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{\textsc{E-Op}} \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{declarations}} \\
\texttt{template <> constexpr } \tau\; f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\overline{x}\texttt{) ... } e \;\mapsto\; f_{\overline{\tau}} = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{an instance} \\
\texttt{template <}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{> requires } \overline{Q}\texttt{ ... } f\texttt{(}\overline{x}\texttt{) ... } e \;\mapsto\; f = \Lambda\overline{\alpha}.\,\lambda\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}.\,\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{a generic function} \\
\texttt{struct } O \texttt{ \{ } \overline{y}\texttt{; ... operator()(}\overline{x}\texttt{) ... } e \texttt{ ... \};} \;\mapsto\; O_{\mathrm{call}} = \lambda\overline{y}.\,\Lambda\overline{\beta}.\,\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{an operation's call function} \\
O \text{ standard} \;\mapsto\; O_{\mathrm{call}} = \lambda().\,o & \text{the standard's function } o \text{ as a constant} \\
\texttt{concept}, \texttt{= delete} \;\mapsto\; \text{nothing} & \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{terms}} \\
\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = f\,\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}\,\overline{\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Call}} \\
\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = f_{\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Trait}, resolved to a specialisation} \\
\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = q.f\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Trait-Assumed}} \\
\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{e_y}\texttt{\}} \rrbracket = (\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket},\; O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket}) & \text{construction} \\
\llbracket e\texttt{(}\overline{e'}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = (\pi_2\,\llbracket e \rrbracket)\,[\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}]\,\overline{\llbracket e' \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Op}: application} \\
\llbracket e\texttt{.}y_i \rrbracket = \pi_i\,(\pi_1\,\llbracket e \rrbracket) & \text{projection} \\
\llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ} = \llbracket f \rrbracket \text{ for a function name } f & \text{the callable of a step} \\
\llbracket e \rrbracket^{\!\circ} = \pi_2\,\llbracket e \rrbracket \text{ for a function object } e & \\
\llbracket \texttt{fold\_left(repeat(}x\texttt{, }n\texttt{), }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} \rrbracket = \llbracket n \rrbracket\,[\sigma]\,(\lambda s.\,\llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ}\,s\,\llbracket x \rrbracket)\,\llbracket a \rrbracket & \text{the numeral} \\

\qquad \text{where } \mathit{step} = \lambda p.\,(\pi_1 p + 1,\; \llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ}\,(\pi_2 p)\,(\pi_1 p)) & \\
\llbracket e_1 \texttt{ ? } e_2 \texttt{ : } e_3 \rrbracket = \mathbf{if}\ \llbracket e_1 \rrbracket\ \mathbf{then}\ \llbracket e_2 \rrbracket\ \mathbf{else}\ \llbracket e_3 \rrbracket & \text{case on } \mathit{Bool} \\
\llbracket \texttt{std::optional\{}e\texttt{\}} \rrbracket = \mathrm{inr}\,\llbracket e \rrbracket \qquad \llbracket \texttt{std::nullopt} \rrbracket = \mathrm{inl}\,() & \\
\end{array}

A trait has no image of its own; its specialisations are closed terms $f_{\overline{\tau}}$, and a generic function that serves as an instance with a context is the function from records to a record that \textsc{E-Shape} builds its dictionary with.
Where \cppinline{size_t} is read as $\mathbb{N}$, $\llbracket n \rrbracket$ is the Church numeral of the value of $n$; the translation of a term with a runtime \cppinline{n} is the term with $n$ as a free variable of numeral type, and Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation} is stated for closed terms.}

\subsection{Theorems}
\label{app:fjlt:theorems}
% Proof sketches for the statements of Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}.  They
% rest on the typing rules (A.2), the evaluation rules (A.3) and the
% translation (A.4), which are still to be written; each sketch names the
% cases it needs from them.  Full proofs and a mechanisation are future work.

The statements are in Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}; the proofs are here, complete relative to the rules of Figures~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} to~\ref{fig:fjlt-translation}, whose omitted cases (constants, pairs, optionals, conditionals, the arithmetic of base types) are the textbook ones.
Throughout, the translation $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket$ is the one of Section~\ref{app:fjlt:translation}, into System~F with products, sums and the base types as constants, which is conservative over System~F proper.
A context translates pointwise: $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$ has a type variable for each $\alpha \in \Delta$ and a record variable $q_Q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket$ for each assumed constraint $Q \in \Delta$, and $\llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket = \overline{x : \llbracket \tau \rrbracket}$.
Three facts about the target are used without proof: System~F is strongly normalising~\cite{girard1989proofs} and confluent, and substitution of types for type variables preserves typing.

\begin{lemma}[Dictionaries are well typed]
\label{lem:dict}
If $\Delta \Vdash Q$, then $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket; \varnothing \vdash \llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket : \llbracket Q \rrbracket$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the entailment derivation, mutually with Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, the induction being on the size of the derivation.
\textsc{E-Assume}: the dictionary is the variable $q_Q$, which $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$ types at $\llbracket Q \rrbracket$.
\textsc{E-Shape}: $Q = C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}$, the dictionary is the record $\{\,\overline{r_i = \llbracket \Delta \Vdash R_i[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}\,\}$, each field has type $\llbracket R_i \rrbracket[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}]$ by the induction hypothesis, and that is the field type of $\llbracket C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket$ by definition.
\textsc{E-Law}: the dictionary is $\mathit{true} : \mathit{Bool}$.
\textsc{E-Op}: the premise types $e$ at $\tau'$ under $\overline{x : \tau_x}$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation} on a smaller derivation $\llbracket e \rrbracket : \llbracket \tau' \rrbracket$ under $\overline{x : \llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket}$, and $\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket : \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau' \rrbracket$; for \cppinline{std::same_as} $\tau' = \tau$, and for \cppinline{std::convertible_to} the translation inserts the conversion, a constant of type $\llbracket \tau' \rrbracket \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$, so the field has type $\overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ in both cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, type preservation]
By induction on the typing derivation, one case per rule.
\textsc{T-Var}: $\llbracket x \rrbracket = x$, and $\llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket$ types it at $\llbracket \tau \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Op}, construction: $\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{e_y}\texttt{\}} \rrbracket = (\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket}, O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket})$; the fields have their types by the induction hypothesis, and $O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket} : \forall\overline{\beta}.\,\overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on the operation's body (for a declared operation) or by the assumption on the standard's function (for a standard one), so the pair has type $\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Op}, application: $\pi_2\,\llbracket e \rrbracket$ has the call function's type, is instantiated at the deduced $\overline{\sigma}$, and the arguments have the parameter types by the induction hypothesis; projection: $\pi_i\,(\pi_1\,\llbracket e \rrbracket)$ has the field's type.
\textsc{T-Call}: $f$ was declared by \textsc{D-Generic}, so $f = \Lambda\overline{\alpha}.\,\lambda\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}.\,\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e_f \rrbracket$ has type $\forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{\llbracket Q \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on that declaration; applying it to $\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}$ gives, by type substitution, $\overline{\llbracket Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket$; the records $\overline{\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}$ have the parameter types by Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}, and the arguments theirs by the induction hypothesis.
\textsc{T-Trait}: the call resolved to a specialisation declared by \textsc{D-Instance}, so $f_{\overline{\sigma}} = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket : \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on that declaration, and the arguments have the right types; if it resolved to a generic function with an entailed context, the case is \textsc{T-Call} with the context's dictionaries.
\textsc{T-Trait-Assumed}: $C\texttt{<}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{>} \in \Delta$, so $q_C : \llbracket C\texttt{<}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{>} \rrbracket$ is in $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$, and the projection $q_C.f$ has the field's type $\overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Fold} over \cppinline{repeat}: $\llbracket n \rrbracket : \mathbb{N}$, so $\llbracket n \rrbracket\,[\sigma] : (\sigma \to \sigma) \to \sigma \to \sigma$; the step $\lambda s.\,\llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ}\,s\,\llbracket x \rrbracket$ has type $\sigma \to \sigma$ since the callable $\llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ} : \sigma \to \tau \to \sigma$ (curried in the target) and $\llbracket x \rrbracket : \tau$; applied to $\llbracket a \rrbracket : \sigma$ the whole has type $\sigma$.
Over \cppinline{iota}, the numeral is used at $\mathbb{N} \times \sigma$ with the pair step, and $\pi_2$ of the result has type $\sigma$.
\textsc{D-Generic}: the body is typed under $\overline{\alpha}, \overline{Q}$ and $\overline{x : \tau_x}$, so by the induction hypothesis $\llbracket e \rrbracket : \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ under type variables $\overline{\alpha}$, record variables $\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}$ and $\overline{x : \llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket}$, and the abstraction has the stated polymorphic type; this is the case that uses \textsc{T-Trait-Assumed}, since the body reaches a trait only through $\Delta$.
\textsc{D-Instance}: the same with an empty $\Delta$; the declaration order makes the induction on declarations well founded.
The omitted rules are the textbook cases for pairs, sums and conditionals.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dictionaries agree with resolution]
\label{lem:resolve}
If $\Delta \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>}$ with $\overline{\sigma}$ concrete, and the requirement $f$ of $C$ at $\overline{\sigma}$ resolves to a specialisation $f_{\overline{\sigma}}$, then $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>} \rrbracket.f = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{x}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = f_{\overline{\sigma}}$ up to $\eta$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The field is built by \textsc{E-Op} from the requirement's expression $e$, in which the call to $f$ is a \textsc{T-Trait} and translates to $f_{\overline{\sigma}}$ applied to the parameters; abstracting the parameters again gives $f_{\overline{\sigma}}$ up to $\eta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Progress]
\label{lem:progress}
A closed well-typed term is a value or takes a step.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the typing derivation.
If some proper subterm in an evaluation position is not a value, the leftmost such subterm steps by the induction hypothesis, and \textsc{S-Ctx} lifts the step.
Otherwise the term is a value, or a redex of one of the following shapes, each of which steps.
A call $f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{v}\texttt{)}$ typed by \textsc{T-Call} or \textsc{T-Trait}: the premise says the call resolves (for \textsc{T-Call}, the constraints are entailed, so resolution finds the generic function or, through its context, a specialisation), and the body it resolves to precedes the call by the declaration order, so \textsc{S-Call} applies; \textsc{T-Trait-Assumed} cannot occur in a closed term, since $\Delta$ is empty.
An application $O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{v_y}\texttt{\}(}\overline{v}\texttt{)}$: \textsc{S-Op} for a declared operation, \textsc{S-Std} for a standard one, whose operations are total by the choice of $O$.
A primitive $c_1 \oplus c_2$: \textsc{S-Prim}, total by the choice of $\oplus$.
A conditional on a constant of type \cppinline{bool}, a projection of a pair or of a constructed object, and a fold whose fuel is a constant: \textsc{S-If}, \textsc{S-Proj}, \textsc{S-Fold}, with \textsc{S-Fold}'s two cases covering every constant.
Preservation along a step, that $e \longrightarrow e'$ keeps the type, is the usual substitution argument and is used below without further comment.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:termination}, termination]
Directly, by a measure that every step decreases.
Assign each declaration its position in the declaration order, and to a closed term the multiset of positions of the declarations named in it together with the fuel constants of its folds.
\textsc{S-Call} and \textsc{S-Op} replace a call to a declaration at position $p$ by a body naming only declarations at positions below $p$ (the declaration order), so the multiset decreases in the multiset ordering; \textsc{S-Fold} decreases a fuel constant by one; the other rules replace a term by a subterm or a constant and decrease the term's size, which is the tie-breaker.
The multiset ordering on a well-founded order is well founded, so no infinite reduction sequence exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:soundness}, type soundness]
Let $e$ be closed and well-typed at a base type.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:progress} and preservation along steps, every term in the reduction sequence from $e$ is closed and well-typed, so each is a value or steps; by Corollary~\ref{cor:termination} the sequence is finite; so it ends in a value $v$, and at a base type a value is a constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation}, simulation]
First, every value translates to a value: constants to constants, function objects to pairs of values, pairs to pairs, optionals to injections.
Then, for the forward half, by cases on the step $e \longrightarrow e'$, showing that $\llbracket e \rrbracket$ and $\llbracket e' \rrbracket$ reduce to a common term; by \textsc{S-Ctx} and the fact that the translation of a context is a context, it suffices to treat the redex.
\textsc{S-Call} to a specialisation: $\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{v}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = f_{\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ $\beta$-reduces to $\llbracket e_f \rrbracket[\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}/\overline{x}] = \llbracket e_f[\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \rrbracket$, the translation commuting with substitution of values.
\textsc{S-Call} to a generic function: $f\,\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}\,\overline{d}\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ reduces by type application and $\beta$-reduction to $\llbracket e_f \rrbracket$ with $\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}$ for $\overline{\alpha}$, $\overline{d}$ for $\overline{q}$ and $\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ for $\overline{x}$; in it every trait read $q.f$ has become $d.f$, a projection of a constant record, which reduces by Lemma~\ref{lem:resolve} to the translation of the specialisation resolution chose for $e_f[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}]$; the result is $\llbracket e_f[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \rrbracket$.
\textsc{S-Op}: $(\pi_2\,(\overline{\llbracket v_y \rrbracket}, O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket v_y \rrbracket}))\,[\overline{\sigma}]\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ reduces by projection, type application and $\beta$-reduction to $\llbracket e_O \rrbracket$ with the fields and arguments substituted, which is $\llbracket e' \rrbracket$.
\textsc{S-Std} and \textsc{S-Prim}: the constant the standard assigns, by the assumption that the primitives' translations compute the standard's functions on constants.
\textsc{S-If} and \textsc{S-Proj}: a case reduction, a projection.
\textsc{S-Fold}: $\overline{k{+}1}\,[\sigma]\,s\,\llbracket a \rrbracket$ and $\overline{k}\,[\sigma]\,s\,\llbracket f\texttt{(}a\texttt{, }v\texttt{)} \rrbracket$ both reduce to $s^{k+1}\,\llbracket a \rrbracket$ with $s = \lambda t.\,\llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ}\,t\,\llbracket v \rrbracket$, since $\llbracket f\texttt{(}a\texttt{, }v\texttt{)} \rrbracket$ reduces to $s\,\llbracket a \rrbracket$; the fuel-zero case is the numeral $\overline{0}$ returning its argument; over \cppinline{iota}, the same with the pair step.
For the converse at a base type: if $e \Downarrow v$ then by the forward half and induction on the sequence $\llbracket e \rrbracket$ and $\llbracket v \rrbracket$ reduce to a common term, and $\llbracket v \rrbracket$ is a constant, so $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} \llbracket v \rrbracket$.
Conversely, if $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} c$ for a constant $c$: by Corollary~\ref{cor:soundness} $e \Downarrow v$ for some $v$, so $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} \llbracket v \rrbracket$ by the half just proved, and $c = \llbracket v \rrbracket$ since System~F is confluent and both are normal forms.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence}, coherence]
Resolution is a partial function by well-formedness: at most one specialisation at a head, no two generic heads matching one instantiation, and contexts well-founded, so the recursion through contexts terminates with one answer.
The entailment rules are syntax-directed, one rule per shape of $Q$ (\textsc{E-Assume} applies only to assumed constraints, which are variables, never concrete), so two derivations of $\Delta \Vdash Q$ have the same shape, and by induction on that shape they build the same dictionary: the same variable, the same record of fields by the induction hypothesis, the same $\mathit{true}$, the same $\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket$ in which every trait call resolved the same way.
Hence $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket$ depends only on $\Delta$ and $Q$, the records in \textsc{T-Call} are determined by the program, and the translation is a function of it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:parametricity}, parametricity]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, a generic FJlt function translates to a closed System~F term of type $\forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{\llbracket Q \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$; Reynolds' abstraction theorem~\cite{reynolds1983types} gives its free theorem, in which the record parameters are ordinary term parameters, so the theorem relates behaviour at two instantiations through the relation between their records.
\end{proof}

The dependencies run one way: preservation and the two lemmas on dictionaries; progress; termination, directly; soundness from progress and termination; simulation, whose converse uses soundness; coherence and parametricity on their own.
What the proofs do not do: they induct over the rules of Figures~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} and~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval} as given, which omit the generic cases, and they take \cppinline{fold_left} over \cppinline{repeat} and \cppinline{iota} as primitives with the evaluation rule of Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval}, trusting the standard library for it as Section~\ref{sec:translation:adequacy} says.
Their mechanisation is future work.
