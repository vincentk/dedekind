\section{Featherweight Jlt}
\label{app:fjlt}

\subsection{Syntax}
\label{app:fjlt:syntax}

FJlt is a subset of \Cpp{23}, parametric in a \emph{signature} $\Sigma$ of carriers.
A carrier is in $\Sigma$ on three conditions: it is \cppinline{std::semiregular}; the operations a program uses on it are \cppinline{constexpr} and total; and it has an image in the target, a constant type with those operations as constants, and an iterator if it is inductive.
The compiler decides semiregularity and that an operation is \cppinline{constexpr}; totality is a reading, since neither a \cppinline{constexpr} declaration nor a successful evaluation shows an operation defined on every input, and it is what Lemma~\ref{lem:progress} needs and what keeps \cppinline{int}'s overflow and division out; the image is a definition.
A type enters $\Sigma$ by meeting the conditions, not by being named here, which is the structural admission the fragment is named for; the carriers this paper's listings use are \cppinline{bool}, \cppinline{size_t}, \cppinline{std::string}, with concatenation and the comparisons as its operations, allocation failure aside, and the finite enumerations a program or the library declares, such as \cppinline{Ternary} and \cppinline{ArrowLaw}, each a finite sum of units with equality.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-syntax} gives the grammar in the style of Featherweight Java: $\overline{\alpha}$ abbreviates a sequence $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$, a construct written over a sequence is repeated for each element, and the separators the host language wants (commas, \cppinline{&&}, semicolons) are implied.
The overbar is also how a parameter pack reads: a variadic declaration is the family of its arities, each a declaration of the grammar, and a fold expression at arity $n$ is the nested term; what the overbar does not give is a statement about all arities at once.
Every FJlt program is a Jlt program, a \Cpp{23} program that keeps the three rules, with the same meaning, provided no law and no \cppinline{static_assert} builds a box (Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:evaluation}).
The converse, for a Jlt program written in this grammar, is the adequacy argument of Section~\ref{sec:translation:adequacy}.
The listings are Jlt programs, and FJlt programs up to the sugar of Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:closure}, with \cppinline{static_assert(e)} read as the claim that the closed term $e$ evaluates to \cppinline{true}; where a listing steps outside the kernel the text says so: Listing~\ref{lst:rule3}'s Collatz chain is finite by fact, not by a condition on $\mathcal{R}$, and Listing~\ref{lst:sites} dereferences an optional in a constant initialiser (Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:closure}).

Metavariables: $\alpha, \beta$ range over type variables, $b$ over the carriers of $\Sigma$, $O$ over the function-object types, the standard templates (\cppinline{std::plus}, \cppinline{std::logical_and}, \ldots) and the operations a program declares, $r$ over ranges, $\oplus$ over the operations of $\Sigma$, total by the second condition: on unsigned carriers addition, subtraction, multiplication and the bitwise operations, which wrap, the comparisons, and division, remainder and the shifts by a literal the carrier can take (a nonzero divisor, a shift below the width); on \cppinline{bool} the logical operations; by a variable, division, remainder and the shifts are partial and not admitted, and the standard function objects $O$ are those of the same operations, $C$ over concept names, the standard ones included (\cppinline{std::semiregular} is the shape Rule~1 requires of every carrier), $S$ over the two standard result-type concepts \cppinline{std::same_as} and \cppinline{std::convertible_to}, $f$ over function names, $x$ over term variables and $c$ over constants.

\paragraph{Reading the grammar.}
A program is a sequence of declarations and a closed term.
A shape is a concept; its requirements are nested shapes, laws (closed \cppinline{bool} terms) and operations with their result types, exact (\cppinline{std::same_as}) or up to conversion (\cppinline{std::convertible_to}).
A constraint may be negated, \cppinline{!C<T>}, as a \cppinline{requires} clause can write it; a negation carries no evidence, and at concrete types it holds where $C$ does not (\textsc{E-Not}).
A trait is a function template declared and deleted, indexed by a carrier and a value of the operation; an instance is an explicit specialisation of it at a carrier; an instance with a context is a generic function whose \cppinline{requires} clause is the context.
A generic function is a \cppinline{constexpr} function template with a \cppinline{requires} clause and a single \cppinline{return}; \cppinline{auto} in a listing names the type the body has, and a parameter written \cppinline{const τ} or \cppinline{const τ&} is the parameter $\tau\; x$ of the grammar, since a value is immutable and a reference to one denotes it.
The standard function objects and the operations a program declares (a struct with fields and a call operator, itself generic where the listing writes \cppinline{auto}; a former such as \cppinline{Compose} is one) are the only function objects; a term applies one by constructing it, with its fields, and reads a field with \cppinline{.}; the call operator is \cppinline{const}, so applying a function object observes it, as Rule~1 requires of a value.
Terms are variables, constants, function names, calls with explicit or deduced type arguments, applications, arithmetic and comparisons, conversions between carriers, conditionals, pairs, sums and boxed values.
A sum is a type of a family $\mathcal{S}$ that, like $\mathcal{R}$ below, is defined by conditions: two injections $\iota_1, \iota_2$ and a total case form, \texttt{case(}$s$\texttt{, }$f$\texttt{, }$g$\texttt{)} applying $f$ or $g$ to the injected value, each instance spelling them in \Cpp{}.
\cppinline{optional<τ>} is $1 + \tau$, with $\iota_1$ = \cppinline{nullopt}, $\iota_2\,e$ = \cppinline{optional\{e\}} and the case \cppinline{s.transform(g).value_or(f)}, $f$ the value for the empty one; \cppinline{variant<τ₁, τ₂>} is $\tau_1 + \tau_2$, with $\iota_i\,e$ = \cppinline{variant<τ₁, τ₂>(in_place_index<i-1>, e)}, both alternatives spelled, and the case dispatched on the index, \cppinline{s.index() == 0 ? f(get<0>(s)) : g(get<1>(s))}, each \cppinline{get} guarded by the test, as the library's \cppinline{Copairing} writes it; \cppinline{std::visit} is the special case in which one operation, its call operator entailed at both summands, serves as both branches.
\cppinline{*} on an optional and \cppinline{std::get} on a variant are partial and are not terms; under a guard, or in a constant initialiser, where constant evaluation refuses the empty case, they are the case form.
A box is built and read, and nothing else: there is no term for \cppinline{get}, \cppinline{use_count} or \cppinline{==} between boxes, so no program can ask which box holds a value, and that absence is what makes a box a value.
There are two recursion primitives, one per kind of finiteness.
\cppinline{fold_left} folds over a range of fixed length, a value $r$ of a family $\mathcal{R}$ that, like $\Sigma$, is defined by conditions and not by a list: $r$ has a length $|r| \in \mathbb{N}$, fixed before the fold starts, and, when $|r| > 0$, a head and a tail $\mathit{next}\,r$ with $|\mathit{next}\,r| = |r| - 1$; \textsc{S-Fold} consults $|r|$ before it asks for either, and on both sides both are extended to the empty range, the head by the type's default value and the tail by the range itself, which the iterator never reaches, since it runs $|r|$ times.
The default value $\mathit{default}_\tau$ of a type is given by its structure, not by \cppinline{T{}}: a carrier's own default, a pair of defaults, the empty optional, the first injection of a default, a fieldless function object, and for a box a box of its content's default, so that no term of the kernel holds a null box.
Each instance $R$ of $\mathcal{R}$ over an element type $\tau$ contributes to the target a state type $\llbracket R \rrbracket$, a constructor constant $c_R$ applied to the translations of its arguments, and its operations $|\cdot| : \llbracket R \rrbracket \to \llbracket \texttt{size\_t} \rrbracket$, $\mathit{head} : \llbracket R \rrbracket \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ and $\mathit{next} : \llbracket R \rrbracket \to \llbracket R \rrbracket$, constants for a base range and terms over the parts for a range built on another; the state of \cppinline{n >> chain(prior)} is $\llbracket N \rrbracket$, the count, that of \cppinline{transform(r, g)} is $\llbracket R \rrbracket \times \llbracket g \rrbracket$, the arrow carried so that $\mathit{head}$ applies it and $\mathit{next}$ keeps it, and that of \cppinline{iota(i, j)} is $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$.
The kernel's ranges are these.
\cppinline{n >> chain(prior)} is the chain of a count under Figure~\ref{fig:structures}'s $\mathit{prior}$.
Its head is \cppinline{prior(n)} and its tail \cppinline{prior(n) >> chain(prior)}.
It is empty where \cppinline{prior(n)} is nothing, and finite because the step descends (Section~\ref{sec:posture}).
\cppinline{transform(r, g)} is the image of a range under an arrow, Listing~\ref{lst:rule3}'s \cppinline{r >> g}.
Its head is \cppinline{g(head r)}, its tail \cppinline{transform(next r, g)}, and its length $|r|$.
\cppinline{iota(i, j)}, with $i \le j$, is the naturals from $i$ below $j$, with head $i$ and tail \cppinline{iota(i + 1, j)}.
The condition $i \le j$ is the standard view's: reversed endpoints are undefined behaviour, not an empty range.
A range's operations are the terms \texttt{size(}$r$\texttt{)}, \texttt{head(}$r$\texttt{)} and \texttt{next(}$r$\texttt{)}, written $|r|$, $\mathit{head}\,r$ and $\mathit{next}\,r$ in the rules, typed by \textsc{T-Range} at the range's own type $R\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>}$ and total: on the empty range $\mathit{head}$ is the default value of $\tau$, which Rule~1's semiregularity supplies, and $\mathit{next}$ is the range itself (\textsc{S-Range}), values \textsc{S-Fold} never reaches; in \Cpp{} the length is \cppinline{ranges::size}, the head \cppinline{*ranges::begin} under a guard on the length, as \cppinline{*} on an optional is admitted (Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:closure}), and the tail has no standard spelling at the same type, so it is the instance's own.
A range may also be a type variable $R$ under the shape $\mathit{IsRange}\texttt{<}R\texttt{, }T\texttt{>}$ $=$ \cppinline{requires (const R r) { { size(r) } -> same_as<size_t>; { head(r) } -> same_as<T>; { next(r) } -> same_as<R>; }}, which the instances of $\mathcal{R}$ over $\tau$, and only they, satisfy at $T = \tau$ by \textsc{E-Shape}, each operation a field and the family's conditions, that the length falls by one along $\mathit{next}$, the premise, so a type with the three operations and no such law does not satisfy it; Listing~\ref{lst:rule3}'s \cppinline{concat} writes the weaker standard constraint \cppinline{std::ranges::input_range<R>}, which the kernel reads as $\mathit{IsRange}$ with $T$ the deduced \cppinline{ranges::range_value_t<R>}, so that the listing is in the kernel at the kernel's own ranges, and a standard range outside $\mathcal{R}$, an unsized view, is not.
\cppinline{cata(alg, t)} folds over a term: an operation whose fields are terms is a node, a term is a finite composition of nodes, and the fold applies the algebra to each node and the folded values of its legs, one node at a time.
A pair's projections are written as the standard writes them, \cppinline{e.first} and \cppinline{e.second}, and read as $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$~\cite{pierce1991basic}; the library's \cppinline{π_1} and \cppinline{π_2} are the same projections as function objects, applications under \textsc{T-Op}.
The type formers are productions, not members of $\Sigma$, because the translation acts on them structurally: pairs as products, optionals as sums, boxes as unary products; a former enters when it has such an image, as \cppinline{std::variant} does as a sum, above, and \cppinline{std::vector} would as lists with their fold, which is absent because no listing needs it.

\subsection{Typing}
\label{app:fjlt:typing}

Three judgments, in the usual shape.
$\Delta \Vdash Q$ says that the constraint $Q$ is \emph{entailed} in the context $\Delta$, which holds type variables and assumed constraints.
$\Delta; \Gamma \vdash e : \tau$ types a term.
$\vdash D$ says a declaration is well formed, and a program is well formed when its declarations are and its closed term has a type.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} gives the rules that carry weight; the ones for constants, pairs, boxes, conditionals and the arithmetic of base types are the obvious ones and are omitted.

\paragraph{Resolution.}
A trait call $f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}$ at concrete types \emph{resolves} to a declaration of $f$ in two steps, as \Cpp{} resolves an overloaded template: first the most specific of the generic functions whose head matches $\overline{\tau}$ and whose context is entailed, the deleted primary among them, then, if that is the primary, its explicit specialisation at $\overline{\tau}$ if there is one.
The deleted primary never resolves, which is how an operation nobody registered fails a shape.
Well-formedness requires resolution to be a function, in two cases.
At concrete types, no two specialisations share a head, and among the generic functions whose head matches and whose context is entailed there is a unique most specific one, by head and then by context, as \Cpp{} orders constrained templates; \Cpp{} rejects the program where there is none.
At a type variable, in a body with context $\Delta$, a call resolves only when its choice survives every instantiation of the body: every candidate, explicit specialisations included, whose head unifies with the call's types and that would be more specific than the chosen one is excluded by a constraint $\Delta$ decides, entailing it or its negation.
That refines GHC's rule for overlapping instances~\cite{ghc2024overlapping}, which looks at instance heads only and refuses to commit to an instance the types do not yet decide; \Cpp{} does not hold it, and a call it would refuse is a body that varies with a type through a constraint it did not declare, a breach of Rule~2, which the repository's tool reports where it can see one (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
Well-formedness also requires that the declarations, taken at their type arguments, be well founded under \emph{depends on}: a shape at some types depends on the traits its requirements call and on its nested shapes, at those types; a trait at some types on the context of every candidate whose head matches there, which resolution asks even of a candidate it then rejects, and on the candidate it picks; an instance, a generic function or an operation at some types on the shapes of its context, on the declarations its body calls, on the operations its body constructs, and, for an operation, on the operations in its field, parameter and result types, each at the types it names.
The relation is on instantiations and not on bare declarations, since a structured instance may ask for the shape that asks for it, at a smaller type: \cppinline{IsMonoid} at a pair calls \cppinline{unit} there, whose instance asks \cppinline{IsMonoid} at the components (Section~\ref{sec:translation:contexts}); the measure that falls is the pair of depth of the type arguments and declaration position.
Resolution, entailment, \textsc{E-Not}'s negative premise and \textsc{E-Law}'s evaluation all recurse along that relation, so a well-founded relation makes the rules define one relation and makes resolution terminate; \Cpp{} leaves a satisfaction that depends on itself to a rule of its own, and the condition makes it unnecessary.
Two syntactic conditions suffice, and the repository's tool reads the second: a context on a structured head mentions only proper parts of its type arguments, so that each step descends by the head, compare the Paterson conditions~\cite{sulzmann2007understanding}; and the context of an instance whose head has no proper part at a slot, a type variable or a concrete type there, mentions only shapes declared before the trait's primary, so that a shape calling the trait is never asked for while the trait is resolved.
A trait's specialisations may follow the shapes that call it, as Listing~\ref{lst:rule2}'s do, since a specialisation depends on the shape only through its body or its context.
With one exception, a body calls only declarations it depends on, so the defined names of the translation are acyclic; and a term type is required strictly positive: in the translated field types of its nodes the type occurs only directly, never under the domain of a function type, which the iterator and the normalisation results need.
A function may call itself at the same instantiation on a value that visibly descends: the argument in the slot of a parameter $p$, of an unsigned carrier or of a type variable under a shape the kernel reads as \cppinline{IsCountWord}, whose fields are the operations the body uses and a coercion \cppinline{{ size_t(n) } -> same_as<size_t>} that preserves the count, so a count no wider than \cppinline{size_t}, is $p / c$ with $c \geq 2$, $p \gg c$ with $c \geq 1$, or $p - 1$, under a test of $p$ against $0$ whose branch returns without the call, Rule~3's step down, as Listing~\ref{lst:laws}'s \cppinline{power} halves its count \cppinline{n} of type \cppinline{N}.
The exception translates to a fold: along any path of calls each takes $p$ to a smaller value and none is made at $0$, so a path has at most $p + 1$ calls, the last for the base case, and $f = \lambda\overline{x}.\, (\mathit{iter}\; (\mathit{toNat}\,p + 1)\; F\; d)\; \overline{x}$ over all its parameters $\overline{x}$, $p$ among them, the count read into the target's naturals by the carrier's coercion or the shape's field, where the successor does not wrap, with $F = \lambda f.\,\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket$ the body abstracted over the function its self-call names, a self-call at the same instantiation translating to $f\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket}$ on that binder, with no type arguments and no dictionaries, which are those of the enclosing abstraction, and $d$ the constant function to $\mathit{default}$ of the result type (Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:syntax}), which at a type variable is the one field of \cppinline{std::semiregular}'s record that the translation uses, and which no evaluation reaches.
So the translation needs no recursive binding, and the only recursion in a program is that of the two folds.
The exception is what admits the binary method of Listing~\ref{lst:laws}, whose recursion is on a count; the library's \cppinline{cata} over typed terms, whose recursion is on the type of the term and not on a value, is the kernel's primitive \cppinline{cata} spelled as a generic function, sugar in the sense of Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:closure}.

\paragraph{Reading the rules.}
\textsc{E-Law} is where a law is a value: entailment evaluates it, so a shape with a law is satisfied only at instances whose certificate holds, and the stratification of laws (closed terms of base type) keeps typing and evaluation from chasing each other.
Its premise is closed because evaluation is defined on closed terms only: a law is evaluated where a record is built, at concrete types, and at a type variable a law is entailed only through an assumed shape that lists it, by \textsc{E-Law-Assumed}, as an operation is through \textsc{T-Req-Assumed}.
The side condition of \textsc{E-Shape} makes entailment syntax-directed: an assumed shape is its record variable and is never rebuilt field by field, which is what Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence} needs.
\textsc{E-Op} reads $S$ as equality for \cppinline{std::same_as} and as conversion for \cppinline{std::convertible_to}; in the second case \textsc{T-Req-Assumed} lets a body use the result only at the declared $\tau$, so the conversion the dictionary makes once, in the field, is the conversion the source would make at every use, and a body that used the unconverted result, say to compare two of them at their own type, would be using an operation the constraint did not name, which the typing refuses and \Cpp{} accepts: the one place FJlt is stricter than the compiler.
The library needs the conversion form where a requirement's expression is an lvalue of the wanted type (\cppinline{const T&} is not \cppinline{same_as<T>}; \cppinline{IsPointed}'s unit), where a step returns a proxy for the carrier (\cppinline{HasNNOStep}'s successor), or where an object is presented by another type (\cppinline{Presents}); in each, the body uses the result at the declared type.
The requirements of a shape, in \textsc{T-Req-Assumed} and \textsc{E-Law-Assumed}, are its own and, transitively, those of its nested shapes, so that an assumed \cppinline{IsCommutativeMonoid<T, Op>} yields the operations and the associativity law of \cppinline{IsMonoid<T, Op>}; the dictionary projects along the path of nested fields, $q_C.m.r$ for a requirement $r$ of the nested shape held in field $m$.
When more than one assumed requirement matches an operation, the rules take the leftmost shape of the \cppinline{requires} clause and, within it, the first matching requirement in declaration order, so both rules are functions of the body; once instantiated the candidates denote one operation, by Lemma~\ref{lem:resolve}, and the choice fixes which is projected before that.
\textsc{T-Req-Assumed} is the rule that makes \textsc{D-Generic} a definition check: inside a constrained body, an operation on a constrained type is typed only as an instance of a requirement of a constraint in $\Delta$, \cppinline{Op{}(x, x)} as the pattern \cppinline{{ Op{}(a, b) }} at $a, b := x$, and \cppinline{unit<T>(Op{})} as the pattern \cppinline{{ unit<T>(Op{}) }}, whose parameters are the variables of the \cppinline{requires} clause free in it, here none; a trait is reachable the same way.
A variable of a fieldless function-object type denotes that type's one value, so \cppinline{op} of type \cppinline{Op} is \cppinline{Op{}} and \cppinline{op(a, b)} matches the pattern \cppinline{{ Op{}(a, b) }}, which is how Listing~\ref{lst:rule3}'s \cppinline{concat} applies its \cppinline{op}.
A premise $f : \sigma \times \tau \to \sigma$ in \textsc{T-Fold} and \textsc{T-Case} says that $f$ is a function name or a function object whose application to arguments of those types is typed at $\sigma$ by \textsc{T-Call}, \textsc{T-Trait}, \textsc{T-Op}, \textsc{T-App} or, at a type variable, \textsc{T-Req-Assumed}, as the pattern of an assumed requirement.
\textsc{T-Conv} types the conversion between carriers that \Cpp{} inserts implicitly and the kernel term writes, $\tau\texttt{(}e\texttt{)}$, total by the condition on conversions of Table~\ref{tab:closure:decl}; write $\tau^{S}\texttt{(}e\texttt{)}$ for $e$ when $S$ is \cppinline{std::same_as} and for $\tau\texttt{(}e\texttt{)}$ when it is \cppinline{std::convertible_to}, so that \textsc{T-Req-Assumed} types $\tau^{S}$ of the pattern and a use of the requirement keeps its declared type when \textsc{S-Call} substitutes the instance (\textsc{S-Conv}).
The range rule types a range of $\mathcal{R}$ by its instance's conditions, \cppinline{iota(i, j)} only at $i \le j$, and \textsc{S-Fold} preserves them, since $\mathit{next}$ does.
The conditions are semantic, that a step descends or that two bounds are ordered, so a chain under a step that does not descend is ill-typed, and termination of a fold is in that measure assumed in the typing of its range.
\textsc{T-Fold-R} types a fold at a type variable $R$ through the assumed shape $\mathit{IsRange}\texttt{<}R\texttt{, }\tau\texttt{>}$, as \textsc{T-Req-Assumed} types an operation: the length, head and tail are its three requirements, read from the record, and a caller's concrete range supplies the record by \textsc{E-Shape} at instantiation.
In \textsc{T-Cata}, $\mu F$ is a term type, the inductive type of the nodes that build it, and the algebra is applied to each node together with the folded values of its legs, the node itself still in hand, which is the library's interface (\cppinline{alg(t, cata(alg, leg)...)}) and the paramorphism's.

\paragraph{Inferred laws.}
\label{app:fjlt:inferred}
A law at a construction is entailed, not declared: the law is a trait call, resolution picks the instance whose context the program entails, and \textsc{E-Law} evaluates its certificate, which must be \texttt{true}.
Composed, these give a derived rule, the instance rule of the entailment of qualified types~\cite{jones1994qualified}:
\[
\textsc{(E-Law-Ctx)}\quad \dfrac{\mathit{law}\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \text{ resolves to } \forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{Q} \Rightarrow \mathit{law}\texttt{<}\overline{\tau'}\texttt{>} \qquad \overline{\tau} = \overline{\tau'}[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \qquad \Delta \Vdash \overline{Q}[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \qquad \mathit{law}\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \Downarrow \texttt{true}}{\Delta \Vdash \mathit{law}\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}}
\]
A finite power of a semiring is a semimodule over it so: the library declares the power a product of its base, and one instance, whose context is the base's semiring laws, gives the power's semimodule law, as semimodules over a semiring form a variety, closed under products.
Each step reads the laws of a term's immediate parts only, so the inference is local~\cite{pierce2000local}, and it ends by the conditions on contexts that end instance search~\cite{sulzmann2007understanding}.

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{app:fjlt:evaluation}

Evaluation is small-step and call-by-value on closed terms, $e \longrightarrow e'$, with values and evaluation contexts
\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
v &::=& c \mid f \mid O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{v}\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::pair\{}v\texttt{, }v\texttt{\}} \mid \iota_1\,v \mid \iota_2\,v \mid \texttt{std::optional\{}v\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::nullopt} \\
  &\mid& \texttt{std::make\_shared<const }\tau\texttt{>(}v\texttt{)} \\
E &::=& [\,] \mid f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{v}\texttt{, }E\texttt{, }\overline{e}\texttt{)} \mid E\texttt{(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \mid v\texttt{(}\overline{v}\texttt{, }E\texttt{, }\overline{e}\texttt{)} \mid E \oplus e \mid v \oplus E \mid \tau\texttt{(}E\texttt{)} \mid E \texttt{ ? } e \texttt{ : } e \\
  &\mid& \iota_i\,E \mid \texttt{case(}E\texttt{, }e\texttt{, }e\texttt{)} \mid \texttt{case(}v\texttt{, }E\texttt{, }e\texttt{)} \mid \texttt{case(}v\texttt{, }v\texttt{, }E\texttt{)} \mid \ldots
\end{array}
\]
where the elided contexts are the remaining argument positions of pairs, optionals, boxes, projections, constructions, folds and the two branches of a case, each evaluated left to right.
It is \Cpp{}'s constant evaluation restricted to the grammar, extended by \textsc{S-Unbox} at run time, and Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval} gives the rules; $e \Downarrow v$ abbreviates $e \longrightarrow^{*} v$.
The context grammar picks one redex per non-value term, so the relation is deterministic.

\subsection{Translation into System F}
\label{app:fjlt:translation}

The target is System~F with products, sums, the carriers of $\Sigma$ taken as constants with their total operations, and inductive types with their iterators: the natural numbers with $\mathit{iter} : \forall\sigma.\,\mathbb{N} \to (\sigma \to \sigma) \to \sigma \to \sigma$, reducing by $\mathit{iter}\,(k{+}1)\,s\,z \longrightarrow \mathit{iter}\,k\,s\,(s\,z)$ and $\mathit{iter}\,0\,s\,z \longrightarrow z$, and for each term type $\mu F$ its iterator $\mathit{fold}_F : \forall\sigma.\,(F(\sigma) \to \sigma) \to \mu F \to \sigma$, reducing one constructor at a time, $\mathit{fold}_F\,\beta\,(\mathrm{in}\,n) \longrightarrow \beta\,(F(\mathit{fold}_F\,\beta)\,n)$; the paramorphism, which also hands each node itself to the algebra, is the iterator at $\mu F \times \sigma$ with the node rebuilt beside the result, as usual.
Reduction in the target is full $\beta$-reduction, any redex anywhere, together with projections, case, the $\delta$-rules of the constants, the unfolding of a defined name to its body and the two iterator rules; no evaluation order is imposed, which is the setting of the normalisation and confluence results used below.
Records are nested pairs.
System~F with products, sums and positive inductive types with their iterators is strongly normalising and confluent, and its iterative fragment embeds into System~F by an encoding that preserves reduction~\cite{matthes1999extensions}; for first-order rewrite rules on algebraic constants, Breazu-Tannen and Gallier show that adding them to System~F conserves strong normalisation and confluence~\cite{breazutannen1991polymorphic,breazutannen1994polymorphic}.
These results cover the parts separately, and this appendix assumes their combination, which the same method gives: $\mathit{iter}$ in the accumulator form above is the standard iterator at $\sigma \to \sigma$, each of its steps at least one step of that; the defined names are acyclic, by the declaration order, and expand away; the $\delta$-rules are ground, a constant out of constants, and preserve reducibility; and confluence then follows by Newman's lemma, there being no critical pairs.
That is a sketch, not a proof, and the one-constructor-at-a-time iterators are what make Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation} exact where a Church encoding, unfolding all at once, would not be.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-translation} gives the translation.

\paragraph{Reading the translation.}
A function object is a pair of its fields and its call function, so a fieldless \cppinline{std::plus<size_t>} is $((), \mathit{plus})$, passing it as a step uses its second component, and reading a field uses its first.
A trait has no image of its own; its specialisations are closed terms $f_{\overline{\tau}}$, and a generic function that serves as an instance with a context is the function from records to a record that \textsc{E-Shape} builds its dictionary with.
Where \cppinline{size_t} is read as $\mathbb{N}$, by a coercion constant from the carrier to the inductive naturals, $\llbracket n \rrbracket$ is the natural number that is $n$'s value; the translation of a term with a runtime \cppinline{n} is the term with $n$ as a free variable of type $\mathbb{N}$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation} is stated for closed terms.
The call function of a constrained operation abstracts over its dictionaries as a generic function does, and an application supplies them.
The iterator is the catamorphism of the chain of a natural number, a fold over any range of $\mathcal{R}$ is that catamorphism at the range's length with the range's position carried beside the state, and \cppinline{cata} is the catamorphism of a term's inductive type; a term type translates to $\mu F$, whose constructors are its node operations, and \textsc{T-Node} reads a construction as a term of that type, $\mathrm{in}_O$ of its fields, exactly where a term of type $\mu F$ is expected, the argument of a \cppinline{cata} or of an algebra, so that a term keeps one derivation.
The library's set recursion is the first two, and its one engine over terms is the third.
A box translates to a unary product rather than to nothing, on purpose: a box's only observable is its content, so which box holds it is erased, and the unit component keeps unboxing a step on both sides, which Lemma~\ref{lem:forward} needs.
Its one non-local ingredient is the \emph{dictionary} of an entailment: $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket$ is the record that witnesses $Q$, built by the same recursion as resolution.

\subsection{Theorems}
\label{app:fjlt:theorems}

The statements are in Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}; the proofs are here as sketches, relative to the rules of Figures~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} to~\ref{fig:fjlt-translation}, whose omitted cases (constants, pairs, boxes, conditionals, the arithmetic of base types) are the textbook ones; what the sketches leave open is listed after them.
Throughout, $\Sigma$ is any signature meeting the three conditions of Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:syntax}, used only as constants and total operations, and the translation $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket$ is the one of Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:translation}, into System~F with products, sums, the carriers of $\Sigma$ as constants and the iterators.
A context translates pointwise: $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$ has a type variable for each $\alpha \in \Delta$ and a record variable $q_Q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket$ for each assumed constraint $Q \in \Delta$, and $\llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket = \overline{x : \llbracket \tau \rrbracket}$.
Three facts about the target are used without proof: it is strongly normalising and confluent, as above, and substitution of types for type variables preserves typing.

\begin{lemma}[Dictionaries are well typed]
\label{lem:dict}
If $\Delta \Vdash Q$, then $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket; \varnothing \vdash \llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket : \llbracket Q \rrbracket$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the entailment derivation, mutually with Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, the induction being on the size of the derivation.
\textsc{E-Assume}: the dictionary is the variable $q_Q$, which $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$ types at $\llbracket Q \rrbracket$.
\textsc{E-Shape}: $Q = C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}$, the dictionary is the record $\{\,\overline{r_i = \llbracket \Delta \Vdash R_i[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}\,\}$, each field has type $\llbracket R_i \rrbracket[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}]$ by the induction hypothesis, and that is the field type of $\llbracket C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket$ by definition.
\textsc{E-Law} and \textsc{E-Law-Assumed}: the dictionary is $\mathit{true} : \mathit{Bool}$.
\textsc{E-Not}: the dictionary is $() : 1$.
\textsc{E-Op}: the premise types $e$ at $\tau'$ under $\overline{x : \tau_x}$, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation} on a smaller derivation $\llbracket e \rrbracket : \llbracket \tau' \rrbracket$ under $\overline{x : \llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket}$, and $\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket : \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau' \rrbracket$; for \cppinline{std::same_as} there is no conversion, and for \cppinline{std::convertible_to} the field applies the conversion constant $\mathit{conv}_{\tau',\tau} : \llbracket \tau' \rrbracket \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ (\textsc{T-Conv}), so the field has type $\overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ in both cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, type preservation]
By induction on the typing derivation, one case per rule.
\textsc{T-Var}: $\llbracket x \rrbracket = x$, and $\llbracket \Gamma \rrbracket$ types it at $\llbracket \tau \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Construct}: $\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{e_y}\texttt{\}} \rrbracket = (\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket}, O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket})$; the fields have their types by the induction hypothesis, and $O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket e_y \rrbracket} : \forall\overline{\beta}.\,\overline{\llbracket Q \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on the operation's body, typed under $\overline{\beta}, \overline{Q}$ as a generic function's is (for a standard function object, by the assumption on the standard's function), so the pair has type $\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Op} and \textsc{T-App}: $\pi_2\,\llbracket e \rrbracket$ has the call function's type, is instantiated at the deduced $\overline{\sigma}$, receives the dictionaries $\overline{\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\beta}] \rrbracket}$, of the parameter types by Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}, and the arguments, of theirs by the induction hypothesis; \textsc{T-Field}: $\pi_i\,(\pi_1\,\llbracket e \rrbracket)$ has the field's type; \textsc{T-Conv}: the conversion constant has type $\llbracket \tau' \rrbracket \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Inj} and \textsc{T-Case}: an injection translates to the target's, and a case to the target's case with each branch elaborated as a callable at its summand, of type $\sigma$ by the induction hypothesis.
\textsc{T-Call}: $f$ was declared by \textsc{D-Generic}, so $f = \Lambda\overline{\alpha}.\,\lambda\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}.\,\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e_f \rrbracket$ has type $\forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{\llbracket Q \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on that declaration; applying it to $\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}$ gives, by Lemma~\ref{lem:subst}, $\overline{\llbracket Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket$; the records have the parameter types by Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}, and the arguments theirs by the induction hypothesis.
\textsc{T-Trait}: the call resolved to a specialisation declared by \textsc{D-Instance}, so $f_{\overline{\sigma}} = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket : \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on that declaration, and the arguments have the right types; if it resolved to a generic function with an entailed context, the case is \textsc{T-Call} with the context's dictionaries.
\textsc{T-Req-Assumed}: $C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \in \Delta$, so $q_C : \llbracket C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket$ is in $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$, the projection $q_C.r$ has the requirement's field type, the conversion included, $\overline{\llbracket \tau_a \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$, and the arguments have the parameters' types by the induction hypothesis.
\textsc{T-Fold}, for a range of instance $R$ over $\tau$: $\llbracket r \rrbracket = c_R\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} : \llbracket R \rrbracket$ by the induction hypothesis on the arguments and the constructor's type, $\mathit{iter}\,[\llbracket R \rrbracket \times \sigma] : \mathbb{N} \to ((\llbracket R \rrbracket \times \sigma) \to \llbracket R \rrbracket \times \sigma) \to \llbracket R \rrbracket \times \sigma \to \llbracket R \rrbracket \times \sigma$, $\mathit{toNat}\,|\llbracket r \rrbracket| : \mathbb{N}$, and the step has the pair type since $\mathit{next} : \llbracket R \rrbracket \to \llbracket R \rrbracket$, $\mathit{head} : \llbracket R \rrbracket \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ and the callable, elaborated at $\sigma$ and $\tau$ with its type arguments applied and its dictionaries supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:dict}, has type $\sigma \to \tau \to \sigma$ (curried in the target); $(\llbracket r \rrbracket, \llbracket a \rrbracket)$ has the pair type, and $\pi_2$ of the result has type $\sigma$.
\textsc{T-Fold-R}: the same with $q.\mathit{size}$, $q.\mathit{head}$ and $q.\mathit{next}$ for the operations, typed as the fields of $q : \llbracket \mathit{IsRange}\texttt{<}R\texttt{, }\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket$ in $\llbracket \Delta \rrbracket$, the coercion to $\mathbb{N}$ applied to the length.
\textsc{T-Range-Op}: $|\llbracket r \rrbracket|$, $\mathit{head}\,\llbracket r \rrbracket$ and $\mathit{next}\,\llbracket r \rrbracket$ have the operations' types, $\llbracket \texttt{size\_t} \rrbracket$, $\llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ and $\llbracket R \rrbracket$.
\textsc{T-Node}: $\mathit{node}_{O}$ has type $\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket \to \mu F$, by induction on the term type, whose legs are smaller, and it applies to $\llbracket t \rrbracket$ of the node's type by the induction hypothesis.
\textsc{T-Cata}: $\mathit{fold}_F\,[\mu F \times \sigma] : (F(\mu F \times \sigma) \to \mu F \times \sigma) \to \mu F \to \mu F \times \sigma$; $\beta$ has the algebra's type since $\mathrm{in}\,(F\,\pi_1\,n') : \mu F$ rebuilds the node, $F\,\pi_2\,n'$ holds the legs' results of type $\sigma$, and $\llbracket \mathit{alg} \rrbracket^{\!\circ}$, elaborated at $\mu F$ and $\sigma$, has type $\mu F \times \sigma^{k} \to \sigma$ by the induction hypothesis; $\llbracket t \rrbracket : \mu F$ by \textsc{T-Node}, and $\pi_2$ of the result has type $\sigma$.
\textsc{D-Generic}: the body is typed under $\overline{\alpha}, \overline{Q}$ and $\overline{x : \tau_x}$, so by the induction hypothesis $\llbracket e \rrbracket : \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ under type variables $\overline{\alpha}$, record variables $\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}$ and $\overline{x : \llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket}$, and the abstraction has the stated polymorphic type; this is the case that uses \textsc{T-Req-Assumed}, since the body reaches an operation on a constrained type only through $\Delta$.
\textsc{D-Op}: as \textsc{D-Generic}, with the fields among the variables.
\textsc{D-Instance}: the same with an empty $\Delta$; the declaration order makes the induction on declarations well founded, a self-call of the one exception being typed by the declared signature of $f$, at the enclosing type variables and dictionaries, so that the binder of $F$ has the monomorphic type $\overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$ at which the iterator runs, and its body checked once.
The omitted rules are the textbook cases for pairs, sums and conditionals.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Translation commutes with substitution]
\label{lem:subst}
(i) $\llbracket \tau[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket = \llbracket \tau \rrbracket[\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}/\overline{\alpha}]$.
(ii) For a term typed at concrete types, $\llbracket e[\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \rrbracket = \llbracket e \rrbracket[\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}/\overline{x}]$.
(iii) If $\Delta \Vdash Q$ and $\varnothing \Vdash Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}]$, then $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket[\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{d}/\overline{q}] \longrightarrow^{*} \llbracket \varnothing \Vdash Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket$, where $\overline{d}$ are the dictionaries of $\Delta$'s constraints at $\overline{\sigma}$.
(iv) For a term $e$ typed under $\Delta$, $\llbracket e \rrbracket[\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{d}/\overline{q}] \longrightarrow^{*} \llbracket e[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket$, the right side typed at the concrete types.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) By induction on $\tau$: every row of the type translation is structural in the subterms.
(ii) By induction on $e$: every row of the term translation is structural in the subterms, and a variable translates to itself.
(iii) and (iv) together, by induction on the entailment and typing derivations, after Lemma~\ref{lem:resolve}, which they use and which uses only (ii).
\textsc{E-Assume}: the dictionary is $q$, and the substitution puts $d$, the dictionary built at $\overline{\sigma}$, in its place.
\textsc{E-Shape}: the record is rebuilt field by field, each by the induction hypothesis.
\textsc{E-Op}: the field is $\lambda\overline{a}.\,\llbracket \tau^{S}\texttt{(}e_r\texttt{)} \rrbracket$, and $e_r$ is typed under $\Delta$, so (iv) reduces it to the field \textsc{E-Op} builds at $\overline{\sigma}$.
\textsc{E-Law}, \textsc{E-Law-Assumed} and \textsc{E-Not}: the same constant on both sides.
(iv): a \textsc{T-Req-Assumed} site, $q.r\,\ldots$, has become $d.r\,\ldots$ and reduces by Lemma~\ref{lem:resolve} to the resolved instance's translation; the dictionaries of a \textsc{T-Call} or \textsc{T-App} site, and the record of a \textsc{T-Fold-R} site, reduce by (iii); every other row is structural in its subterms.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Dictionaries agree with resolution]
\label{lem:resolve}
If $\varnothing \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>}$ with $\overline{\sigma}$ concrete and $\texttt{\{ }e_r\texttt{ \} -> }S\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>}$ is a requirement of $C$ with parameters $\overline{a}$, then
\[ \llbracket \varnothing \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>} \rrbracket.r = \lambda\overline{a}.\,\llbracket \tau^{S}\texttt{(}e_r[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}]\texttt{)} \rrbracket, \]
in which every call has been resolved; hence for any terms $\overline{e}$,
\[ (\llbracket \varnothing \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>} \rrbracket.r)\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} \longrightarrow^{+} \llbracket \tau^{S}\texttt{(}e_r[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{e}/\overline{a}]\texttt{)} \rrbracket, \]
by a projection and $\beta$-reductions, one per parameter.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The field is built by \textsc{E-Op} as $\lambda\overline{a}.\,\llbracket \tau^{S}\texttt{(}e_r[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}]\texttt{)} \rrbracket$, where the instantiated expression is typed at concrete types, so its calls are \textsc{T-Trait} or \textsc{T-Call} and translate to resolved terms; a projection of the constructed record followed by $\beta$-reduction on $\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket}$, which full $\beta$-reduction permits whether or not the arguments are values, gives the stated term, by Lemma~\ref{lem:subst}~(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Progress]
\label{lem:progress}
A closed well-typed term is a value or takes a step.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on the typing derivation.
If some proper subterm in an evaluation position is not a value, the leftmost such subterm steps by the induction hypothesis, and \textsc{S-Ctx} lifts the step.
Otherwise the term is a value, or a redex of one of the following shapes, each of which steps.
A call $f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{v}\texttt{)}$ typed by \textsc{T-Call} or \textsc{T-Trait}: the premise says the call resolves (for \textsc{T-Call}, the constraints are entailed, so resolution finds the generic function or, through its context, a specialisation), so \textsc{S-Call} applies; \textsc{T-Req-Assumed} cannot occur in a closed term, since $\Delta$ is empty.
An application $O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{}\overline{v_y}\texttt{\}(}\overline{v}\texttt{)}$: \textsc{S-Op} for a declared operation, \textsc{S-Std} for a standard one, whose operations are total by the choice of $O$.
A primitive $c_1 \oplus c_2$: \textsc{S-Prim}, total by the choice of $\oplus$.
A conversion $\tau\texttt{(}c\texttt{)}$: \textsc{S-Conv}, total by the condition on conversions.
A range's operation on a value of a range: \textsc{S-Range}, total by its extension to the empty range.
A conditional on a constant of type \cppinline{bool}, a case on a value of a sum, which by \textsc{T-Case} and canonical forms is an injection, a projection of a pair or of a constructed object, an unbox of a box, a fold whose count is a constant, and a \cppinline{cata} on a node: \textsc{S-If}, \textsc{S-Case}, \textsc{S-Proj}, \textsc{S-Unbox}, \textsc{S-Fold}, \textsc{S-Cata}, with \textsc{S-Fold}'s two cases covering every constant and \textsc{S-Cata} covering every node, leaves included.
Preservation along a step, that $e \longrightarrow e'$ keeps the type, is the usual substitution argument, the conversion written in the kernel term keeping a requirement's use at its declared type after \textsc{S-Call}, and it is used below as source subject reduction.
\end{proof}

Three reductions of the target run ahead of the source: the fuel of a descending self-call, the count of a fold and the reading of a node into its constructor.
Write $t \sqsupseteq e$ when $t$ is $\llbracket e \rrbracket$ with some occurrences of the name $f$ of a function of the one exception, applied to an argument whose value is $w$, replaced by $F^{m}\,d$ with $m \geq w + 1$, some counts $\mathit{toNat}\,|\llbracket r \rrbracket|$ replaced by their numerals, and some readings $\mathit{node}_{O}\,\llbracket v \rrbracket$ of a node that is already a value replaced by their reducts, so that no pending source step is erased or anticipated; in particular $\llbracket e \rrbracket \sqsupseteq e$, and $t \sqsupseteq c$ only for $t = c$, a constant holding neither a name nor a count.

\begin{lemma}[Forward simulation]
\label{lem:forward}
If $t \sqsupseteq e$ and $e \longrightarrow e'$ then $t \longrightarrow^{+} t'$ for some $t' \sqsupseteq e'$, and a value of base type translates to a constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Values: a constant translates to a constant; a function object with fields translates to a pair whose second component, $O_{\mathrm{call}}$ applied to the fields, may still reduce, which is why the claim is made for base types.
Steps, by cases on the rule; by \textsc{S-Ctx} and the fact that the translation of a context is a context with one or more copies of the hole, each of which steps, it suffices to treat the redex.
\textsc{S-Call} to a specialisation: $f_{\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ $\beta$-reduces to $\llbracket e_f \rrbracket[\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}/\overline{x}] = \llbracket e_f[\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \rrbracket$.
\textsc{S-Call} to a generic function: $f\,\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}\,\overline{d}\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ reduces by type application and $\beta$-reduction to $\llbracket e_f \rrbracket$ with $\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}$ for $\overline{\alpha}$, $\overline{d}$ for $\overline{q}$ and $\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ for $\overline{x}$; by Lemma~\ref{lem:subst}~(iv), with $\overline{d}$ the records \textsc{E-Shape} built for the closed call, the result reduces further to $\llbracket e_f[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \rrbracket$, the term the source stepped to, whose requirement uses are typed by \textsc{T-Trait}, under \textsc{T-Conv} where the requirement converts, and whose folds at the former type variable are \textsc{T-Fold}'s at the concrete instance.
\textsc{S-Call} at the self-call of the one exception: where the source calls $f(w)$, $t$ carries $F^{m}\,d$ with $m \geq w + 1$, the first call $f\,v$ having unfolded to $(\mathit{iter}\,(v{+}1)\,F\,d)\,v \longrightarrow^{+} F^{v+1}\,d\,v$; the $\beta$-steps of $F$'s abstractions give the body with $F^{m-1}\,d$ at its self-calls, and $m - 1 \geq w \geq w' + 1$ for the argument $w' < w$ of each, so the result is $\sqsupseteq$ the source's reduct, and $d$ is never reached.
\textsc{S-Op}: $(\pi_2\,(\overline{\llbracket v_y \rrbracket}, O_{\mathrm{call}}\,\overline{\llbracket v_y \rrbracket}))\,[\overline{\sigma}]\,\overline{d}\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ reduces by projection, type application and $\beta$-reduction to $\llbracket e_O \rrbracket$ with fields, dictionaries and arguments substituted, which is $\llbracket e' \rrbracket$ by the same argument as for a generic call.
\textsc{S-Std}, \textsc{S-Prim} and \textsc{S-Conv}: a projection where there is one, then a $\delta$-step to the constant the standard or the declaration assigns, by the assumption that the primitives' translations compute the standard's functions on constants.
\textsc{S-If}, \textsc{S-Case}, \textsc{S-Proj} and \textsc{S-Unbox}: a case reduction, a case reduction to the translation of the selected application, whose evaluation is the next step's, a projection, a projection; the box is a unary product rather than nothing so that unboxing is a step on both sides and the simulation stays strict.
\textsc{S-Fold} with $|r| = k + 1$:
\[ \mathit{iter}\,(k{+}1)\,\mathit{step}\,(\llbracket r \rrbracket, \llbracket a \rrbracket) \longrightarrow \mathit{iter}\,k\,\mathit{step}\,(\mathit{step}\,(\llbracket r \rrbracket, \llbracket a \rrbracket)) \longrightarrow^{+} \mathit{iter}\,k\,\mathit{step}\,(\llbracket \mathit{next}\,r \rrbracket, \llbracket f \rrbracket^{\!\circ}\,\llbracket a \rrbracket\,\llbracket \mathit{head}\,r \rrbracket), \]
the operations $\mathit{next}$ and $\mathit{head}$ computing the range's, and the last term is $\sqsupseteq$ the source's reduct, the fold over $\mathit{next}\,r$ from $f(a, \mathit{head}\,r)$, whose translation differs from it only by the count $\mathit{toNat}\,|\llbracket \mathit{next}\,r \rrbracket|$, which reduces to $k$; the empty case is one step of the iterator followed by a projection.
\textsc{S-Cata}: $\mathit{fold}_F\,\beta\,(\mathrm{in}\,n) \longrightarrow \beta\,(F(\mathit{fold}_F\,\beta)\,n)$ is one step of the term's iterator; $\beta$ then rebuilds the node from the first components, which reduces to $\llbracket n \rrbracket_{\mu F}$ since each leg's pair has that leg, read at $\mu F$, as its first component, and applies $\llbracket \mathit{alg} \rrbracket^{\!\circ}$ to it and to the second components $\pi_2\,(\mathit{fold}_F\,\beta\,\llbracket l_i \rrbracket)$, which are $\llbracket \texttt{cata(}\mathit{alg}\texttt{, }l_i\texttt{)} \rrbracket$; after those projections the term is $\sqsupseteq$ the source's reduct, the node's reading there already reduced.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:termination}, termination]
An infinite reduction $e \longrightarrow e_1 \longrightarrow \cdots$ would give, by Lemma~\ref{lem:forward} from $\llbracket e \rrbracket \sqsupseteq e$, an infinite reduction $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{+} t_1 \longrightarrow^{+} \cdots$ with $t_i \sqsupseteq e_i$ in the target, which is strongly normalising.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:soundness}, type soundness]
Let $e$ be closed and well-typed at a base type.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:progress} and preservation along steps, every term in the reduction sequence from $e$ is closed and well-typed, so each is a value or steps; by Corollary~\ref{cor:termination} the sequence is finite; so it ends in a value $v$, and at a base type a value is a constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation}, simulation]
The first two claims are Lemma~\ref{lem:forward}, the first up to $\sqsupseteq$.
For the converse at a base type: if $e \Downarrow v$ then $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} t \sqsupseteq v$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:forward} and induction on the sequence, and $t = \llbracket v \rrbracket$, a constant.
Conversely, if $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} c$ for a constant $c$: by Corollary~\ref{cor:soundness} $e \Downarrow v$ for some $v$, so $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} \llbracket v \rrbracket$ by the half just proved, and $c = \llbracket v \rrbracket$ since the target is confluent and both are normal forms, the translation being injective on constants.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence}, coherence]
Resolution is a function by well-formedness: at concrete types one specialisation at a head and a unique most specific generic function among the entailed candidates, and at a type variable only a call its context decides, so that the declaration a body's call resolves to is the same at every instantiation of the body and the dictionary built at the type variable is the one built after substitution; contexts are well-founded, so the recursion through contexts terminates with one answer.
\textsc{E-Not} applies only at concrete types, where entailment is decided, and only to a negation not already assumed, as \textsc{E-Shape} applies only to a shape not in $\Delta$, so it adds no choice.
The entailment rules are syntax-directed, exactly one rule per constraint: \textsc{E-Assume} for a constraint in $\Delta$, \textsc{E-Shape} for a shape not in $\Delta$, by its side condition, \textsc{E-Op} for an operation, and \textsc{E-Law} or \textsc{E-Law-Assumed} for a law, which build the same $\mathit{true}$; so two derivations of $\Delta \Vdash Q$ have the same shape, and by induction on that shape they build the same dictionary: the same variable, the same record of fields by the induction hypothesis, the same $\mathit{true}$, the same $\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket$ in which every trait call resolved the same way.
\textsc{T-Req-Assumed} and \textsc{E-Law-Assumed} are deterministic as well, taking the leftmost assumed shape and its first matching requirement in declaration order, nested shapes included, so the translation of a body is a function of the body.
\textsc{T-Req-Assumed}, with \textsc{T-Conv} where its requirement converts, is tried before \textsc{T-Call} and \textsc{T-Trait}, \textsc{T-Node} applies exactly where a term of type $\mu F$ is expected, and \textsc{T-Call} names the declaration resolution selects, so a call has one derivation even where several generic functions of one name have entailed contexts.
Hence $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket$ depends only on $\Delta$ and $Q$, the records in \textsc{T-Call} are determined by the program, and the translation is a function of it.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:parametricity}, parametricity]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, a generic FJlt function translates to a closed System~F term of type $\forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{\llbracket Q \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$; Reynolds' abstraction theorem~\cite{reynolds1983types,wadler1989theorems} gives its free theorem, in which the record parameters are ordinary term parameters, so the theorem relates behaviour at two instantiations through the relation between their records.
The target's constants are interpreted by the identity relation at their base types and its iterators by the least relations closed under $\mathrm{in}$, so the abstraction theorem extends to it, and the term is closed once the defined names are expanded.
\end{proof}

The dependencies run one way: coherence, which makes the translation a function of the term; preservation with the lemma on dictionaries; the substitution lemma, whose third and fourth parts rest on the lemma on resolution; source subject reduction; progress; forward simulation, which uses coherence to translate the reduct; termination from forward simulation and the target's strong normalisation; soundness from progress and termination; the converse of simulation from soundness; parametricity on its own.
What the sketches leave open: they take \cppinline{fold_left} over the ranges of $\mathcal{R}$ as a primitive with the evaluation rule of Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval}, trusting the standard library for \cppinline{iota} (Section~\ref{sec:limits}); the conditions of \textsc{T-Range} are semantic, so termination is in part assumed in the typing; the omitted cases are the textbook ones; and the inductions are on paper, with no mechanisation.
Their mechanisation is future work.

\subsection{The compiler's own functional language}
\label{app:fjlt:ssa}

Instantiation is partial evaluation of the dictionary-passing term at a static type, $\llbracket \mathit{concat} \rrbracket\,[\mathtt{size\_t}]\,d$ with its type and record arguments applied and $\beta$-reduced before the program runs, so the two agree on every closed term: Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation} at the binding time where the records are constants, nothing of a record surviving to run time, and parametricity, a property of the term before that evaluation, holds of every instantiation as an application of it.
For Go, Ellis et al.~\cite{ellis2022generic} prove the dictionary-passing translation correct beside monomorphisation, which Griesemer et al.~\cite{griesemer2020featherweight} proved; \Cpp{} only ever does the second, and the first carries the theorems.
What that evaluation leaves is a closed first-order term over the base types: the type abstractions are gone with their arguments, the record parameters are gone with the records the compiler resolved, every trait call is a call to one specialisation, and the only recursion left is $\mathit{iter}$ and $\mathit{fold}_F$.
The compiler then lowers that term to its intermediate representation, and the representation is in static single assignment form~\cite{cytron1991ssa}: every variable is assigned exactly once, and where two paths of control join, a $\phi$-node names the value that arrives along each.

SSA is a functional language.
Kelsey showed it to correspond to continuation-passing style~\cite{kelsey1995correspondence}, and Appel put the correspondence as a slogan~\cite{appel1998ssa}: a basic block is a local function, its $\phi$-nodes are that function's parameters, a branch is a call that passes the arguments, and dominance is lexical scope.
So a Jlt function is translated twice into a functional language, by this appendix into System~F with records and iterators, and by the compiler into first-order functions with tail calls, with instantiation between the two; Rules~1 and~3 are what makes the source already look like the second.

Listing~\ref{lst:ssa} is a search under a budget as the compiler leaves it before it unrolls the loop: \cppinline{first<Over1000>}, a fold of one step over the first \cppinline{budget} naturals, the state a candidate and whether it has the property, frozen once it has, read out as an optional, here behind a non-template wrapper.
The fold over \cppinline{iota(0, budget)} with that step is one loop, and its header has three $\phi$-nodes: the two components of the state, the candidate and whether it has been found, and the position in the range.
Those are the iterator's state in Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-translation}, the pair $(\llbracket r \rrbracket, \llbracket a \rrbracket)$ with $\llbracket a \rrbracket$ itself a pair, and the count $|r|$, here $\llbracket j \rrbracket - \llbracket i \rrbracket$, is the comparison that ends the loop.
The exit block's two $\phi$-nodes are the fold's result, read out into the optional.
Nothing else survives: not the predicate's concept, not the record its constraint became, not the range object, not the pair; the predicate is the multiplication and the comparison in the loop body, and the budget is the register the wrapper was called with.

This is a reason, not a theorem: the compiler is not among the hypotheses, its lowering is trusted, as every \Cpp{} program trusts it, and the reading of SSA as iterator terms is Appel's, which this paper uses and does not prove.
Since both targets make the loop's state a parameter, the sentence of Section~\ref{sec:binding}, that the discipline costs nothing at run time, has a structural ground: a Jlt fold names in the source the parameter the compiler would otherwise recover from assignments.
One seam shows here too: SSA's values are the scalars in registers, and a value that lives in memory, a boxed value in particular, is read and written through a pointer that no $\phi$-node carries, which is the same line between a value and a cell that Rule~1 draws.

\paragraph{Parallelism, and a branch on the binding time.}
\label{app:fjlt:consteval}
\cppinline{std::reduce} may regroup and reorder its operands, so its result is determined only for an operation that \cppinline{IsCommutativeMonoid} certifies, and Rule~1 gives the absence of data races its parallel policies ask.
A parallel \cppinline{concat} is then one more overload, a run-time one, since the policies' overloads are not \cppinline{constexpr}.

\cppinline{if consteval}, and an \cppinline{if} on \cppinline{std::is_constant_evaluated()}, are how a program can observe its binding time, and FJlt need not have them.
\cppinline|if consteval { A } else { B }| desugars to \cppinline{A}, provided \cppinline{B} computes the same value as \cppinline{A} on every input, so that the program cannot observe the difference after all (Table~\ref{tab:closure}).\footnote{The choice of \cppinline{A} is not arbitrary: it is the branch constant evaluation takes, which is FJlt's evaluation, while \cppinline{B} is typically not constant-evaluable, which is why the program branches at all.}
For the parallel \cppinline{concat} the condition is the free monoid's universal property: a fold over a concatenation is the operation applied to the folds of the parts, by associativity, and commutativity lets the parts come in any order.
So the law that licenses the shortcut also proves that the branches agree.
For an arbitrary branch the condition is semantic, and a reader holds it, as for the part of Rule~2 the linter does not reach.

\paragraph{Lambdas.}
A lambda \cppinline{[c...](const τ x...) { return e; }} whose captures are FJlt values taken by copy and whose body is an FJlt term under those captures and parameters is the operation struct with the captures as fields and \cppinline{e} as the body of its call operator.
A generic lambda written with a template head and a \cppinline{requires} clause is the operation with a generic call operator.
The reason is the standard's own definition of the closure type: a class with the copied captures as members and a \cppinline{const} call operator, which is the operation production of Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-syntax} to the letter.
Capture by reference is excluded by Rule~1 before this lemma is reached.
A capturing closure type is copy constructible and neither default constructible nor assignable, so it is not \cppinline{std::semiregular} and Rule~1 does not admit it as a value; it is admitted as sugar, judged through its image, which is semiregular when its captures are.
That is the general rule for sugar: the rules are asked of the kernel term.
A closure type has no name, so a lambda is never a carrier and never the head of an instance, and nothing in the statement asks to look inside it once built.

\paragraph{\texttt{if constexpr} on a value.}
A body \cppinline{if constexpr (p) return A; else return B;} with \cppinline{p} a closed \cppinline{bool} term, a law or a trait read, and \cppinline{A}, \cppinline{B} FJlt terms of one type under the same context, is the term \cppinline{p ? A : B}.
The condition is stronger than \Cpp{} asks, since \Cpp{} does not instantiate the discarded branch and FJlt types both, and the compiler decides it: the rewritten body compiles or it does not.
Constant evaluation of the two spellings agrees because both evaluate \cppinline{p} first and then one branch.
Whether the two branches share a type is decided per site by the rewrite, and a site whose branches differ in type, \cppinline{lattice_term}'s \cppinline{Meet<RA, RB>} against \cppinline{Meet<RB, RA>}, is not this row but a branch on types in a value's clothing, and waits with the next.

\paragraph{\texttt{if constexpr} on a constraint.}
A body \cppinline{if constexpr (C<T>) return A; else return B;} in a generic function with head $\overline{Q}$, where \cppinline{A} is an FJlt term under $\overline{Q}, C\texttt{<T>}$ and \cppinline{B} one under $\overline{Q}, \texttt{!}C\texttt{<T>}$, is the pair of generic functions with those two heads.
The pair plays the role of a two-clause instance chain of Morris and Jones~\cite{morris2010instance}, an explicit alternation decided by one constraint, though here resolution by specificity (Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:typing}) rather than clause order picks its branch: at a concrete type the compiler decides $C\texttt{<T>}$, and at a type variable the call resolves only where the enclosing context decides it.
A call it cannot decide is the next row's.
When the test is \cppinline{std::same_as<T, X>} at a concrete \cppinline{X} and \cppinline{T} is the whole head, the first function is an explicit specialisation at \cppinline{X}.
A test of a \cppinline{requires} expression written in the body is not a declared constraint under Rule~2, and the tool's \texttt{inline-requires} check holds the module at zero.

\paragraph{A call its clause does not decide.}
A generic function whose body calls a chain at its own type variable, without a clause that entails the chain's constraint or its negation, varies with the type through a constraint it did not declare.
\Cpp{} accepts it and resolves the call at each instantiation; the kernel refuses it, by GHC's rule.
Its desugaring is constraint inference: the call's resolution becomes a parameter of the enclosing function, which each caller supplies after resolving it at a concrete type, where resolution is a function, so the kernel term agrees with what \Cpp{} instantiates.
The price is in the free theorem, which now ranges over that parameter.
Written by hand, the same thing is to decide the constraint in the clause, by requiring it or by splitting on it.

\paragraph{Sums.}
The kernel has the case form (Appendix~\ref{app:fjlt:syntax}), so given FJlt terms \cppinline{f} and \cppinline{g} on the two summands their copairing is an FJlt term, \texttt{case(}$s$\texttt{, }$f$\texttt{, }$g$\texttt{)}.
\cppinline{has_value} and \cppinline{value_or} on an optional are that case on $1 + T$, and \cppinline{std::visit(O{}, v)} is the case whose branches are one generic call operator entailed at each summand.
\cppinline{*} on an optional is partial, undefined on the empty one, and is admitted under a \cppinline{has_value} guard, which desugars to the case form, as the head of a range is admitted only when its length is positive, or in a constant initialiser, where constant evaluation refuses the empty case and the compiler holds the guard, as for Listing~\ref{lst:sites}'s \cppinline{*automaton(sites)}.

\paragraph{Variable and class templates.}
A variable template \cppinline{v<T...> = e} is the nullary function template \cppinline{v<T...>() { return e; }}, and a class template with a static constant member is the same, since constant evaluation of the three agrees.
A partial specialisation over a family, every \cppinline{Rational<I>} under \cppinline{+}, is an instance with a context, in the grammar.
Nothing is missing for one family; a primary that answers \cppinline{false} beside families that answer \cppinline{true} is two generic functions whose heads both match, which resolution by specificity orders.
The host's lookup wall (Section~\ref{sec:limits}) makes the FJlt spelling unwritable in the library's module layout; the library's spelling is FJlt by this statement.

\paragraph{Member functions.}
A \cppinline{const} member function \cppinline{τ m(τ x...) const { return e; }} of an operation is the generic function \cppinline{m(O self, τ x...)} whose body reads the fields of \cppinline{self} where the member read \cppinline{this}.
A static member function is a generic function.
A struct with fields and no call operator is a product, nested pairs.
Rule~1 supplies the \cppinline{const}, and the body is FJlt by hypothesis, so nothing is missing.

\paragraph{Abbreviations.}
An alias template is substitution.
\cppinline{std::tuple} of FJlt types is nested pairs.
\cppinline{std::array<T, n>} is a product of $n$ copies and also a member of $\mathcal{R}$, with length $n$, a head and a tail, so a fold over it is already in the grammar.

\paragraph{Overloading.}
Overloads of one name at distinct concrete carriers are explicit specialisations of one trait.
Constrained overloads are generic functions, and where their heads both match, resolution by specificity orders them, as for Listing~\ref{lst:laws}'s overloads of \cppinline{fold} and \cppinline{concat}.

\paragraph{Inheritance.}
Jlt has no subtyping: the grammar has no base clause and no derived-to-base conversion, a class with a base that holds state or a virtual function is outside the posture, and the tool's \texttt{inheritance} check holds the module at zero.
What the library writes with a base clause is a registration, \cppinline{struct is_involutive<X> : std::true_type}, every base an empty class: the family row spelled by inheritance, read as the fact it states.

\paragraph{What the table says.}
The table is complete by measurement, not by argument, at the resolution of a syntax node's kind: the repository's tool lists every kind the \texttt{category} module and the listings write, leaving out the compiler's implicit wrappers, and each is the kernel's or a row of the table.
A kind does not tell every use apart: a binary operator is arithmetic or an assignment, and the assignments are the row of local state, which Rule~1's footnote admits and which reading, not counting, finds.
With the table closed, the second direction of Section~\ref{sec:translation:adequacy} reads: a Jlt program built from the grammar by these constructions under their conditions is an FJlt program.
