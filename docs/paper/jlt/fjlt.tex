\section{Featherweight Jlt}
\label{app:fjlt}
% Design (decided on #981): no overlapping instances; fold and explicit fuel as
% the only recursion; laws as ordinary values; multi-parameter instances with
% contexts; associated types and same-type constraints as in F^G; instance

\subsection{Syntax}
\label{app:fjlt:syntax}

FJlt is a subset of \Cpp{23}.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-syntax} gives its grammar in the style of Featherweight Java: $\overline{\alpha}$ abbreviates a sequence $\alpha_1, \ldots, \alpha_n$, a construct written over a sequence is repeated for each element, and the separators the host language wants (commas, \cppinline{&&}, semicolons) are implied.
Every FJlt program is a \Cpp{23} program with the same meaning; the converse fails by design.
The listings of Section~\ref{sec:translation} are FJlt programs, with \cppinline{static_assert(e)} read as the claim that the closed term $e$ evaluates to \cppinline{true}.

Metavariables: $\alpha, \beta$ range over type variables, $b$ over base types (\cppinline{bool}, \cppinline{size_t}), $O$ over the function-object types, the standard templates (\cppinline{std::plus}, \cppinline{std::logical_and}, \ldots) and the operations a program declares, $r$ over ranges, $\oplus$ over the arithmetic and comparison operators of the base types, $C$ over concept names, the standard ones included (\cppinline{std::semiregular} is the shape Rule~1 requires of every carrier), $S$ over the two standard result-type concepts \cppinline{std::same_as} and \cppinline{std::convertible_to}, $f$ over function names, $x$ over term variables and $c$ over constants.

\caption{The syntax of FJlt, a subset of \Cpp{23}.
A program is a sequence of declarations and a closed term.
A shape is a concept; its requirements are nested shapes, laws (closed \cppinline{bool} terms) and operations with their result types, exact (\cppinline{std::same_as}) or up to conversion (\cppinline{std::convertible_to}).
A trait is a function template declared and deleted, indexed by a carrier and a value of the operation; an instance is an explicit specialisation of it at a carrier; an instance with a context is a generic function whose \cppinline{requires} clause is the context.
A generic function is a \cppinline{constexpr} function template with a \cppinline{requires} clause and a single \cppinline{return}; \cppinline{auto} in a listing names the type the body has.

Terms are variables, constants, function names, calls with explicit or deduced type arguments, applications, arithmetic and comparisons, conditionals, pairs and optionals.
\cppinline{std::ranges::fold_left} is the one recursion primitive, over a range whose length is fixed before the fold starts: \cppinline{repeat(x, n)}, $n$ copies of $x$, or \cppinline{iota(0, n)}, the first $n$ naturals.}

\subsection{Typing}
\label{app:fjlt:typing}

Three judgments, in the usual shape.
$\Delta \Vdash Q$ says that the constraint $Q$ is \emph{entailed} in the context $\Delta$, which holds type variables and assumed constraints.
$\Delta; \Gamma \vdash e : \tau$ types a term.
$\vdash D$ says a declaration is well formed, and a program is well formed when its declarations are and its closed term has a type.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} gives the rules that carry weight; the ones for constants, pairs, optionals, conditionals and the arithmetic of base types are the obvious ones and are omitted.

\paragraph{Resolution.}
A trait call $f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}$ at concrete types \emph{resolves} to a declaration of $f$: the explicit specialisation at $\overline{\tau}$ if there is one, otherwise the generic function whose head matches $\overline{\tau}$ and whose context is entailed, recursively.
The deleted primary never resolves, which is how an operation nobody registered fails a shape.
Well-formedness requires resolution to be a partial function: no two specialisations at one head, and no two generic functions whose heads both match an instantiation (the no-overlap condition); and it requires every context to mention only proper parts of the head's type arguments, so that resolution terminates~\cite{sulzmann2007understanding}.

\footnotesize
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\textsc{(E-Assume)}\quad \dfrac{Q \in \Delta}{\Delta \Vdash Q}
\qquad
\textsc{(E-Law)}\quad \dfrac{\Delta; \varnothing \vdash e : \texttt{bool} \qquad e \Downarrow \texttt{true}}{\Delta \Vdash e}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(E-Shape)}\quad \dfrac{\texttt{concept } C \texttt{ = } \overline{R} \qquad \Delta \Vdash R_i[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}] \text{ for each } i}{\Delta \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>}}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(E-Op)}\quad \dfrac{\Delta; \overline{x : \tau_x} \vdash e : \tau' \qquad \tau' \mathrel{S} \tau}{\Delta \Vdash \texttt{requires (}\overline{\texttt{const }\tau_x\; x}\texttt{) \{ \{ }e\texttt{ \} -> }S\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>; \}}}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(T-Var)}\quad \dfrac{x : \tau \in \Gamma}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash x : \tau}
\qquad
\textsc{(T-Op)}\quad \dfrac{O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} : \overline{\sigma} \to \sigma \text{ (the standard's signature)} \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash \overline{e : \sigma}}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{\}(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} : \sigma}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(T-Call)}\quad \dfrac{f : \forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{Q} \Rightarrow \overline{\tau_x} \to \tau \qquad \Delta \Vdash \overline{Q}[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash \overline{e : \tau_x[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}]}}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} : \tau[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}]}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(T-Trait)}\quad \dfrac{f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>} \text{ resolves to a declaration of type } \overline{\tau_x} \to \tau \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash \overline{e : \tau_x}}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} : \tau}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(T-Trait-Assumed)}\quad \dfrac{C\texttt{<}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{>} \in \Delta \qquad f \text{ is a requirement of } C \text{ at } \overline{\tau_x} \to \tau \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash \overline{e : \tau_x}}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash f\texttt{<}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} : \tau}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(T-Fold)}\quad \dfrac{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash r : \texttt{range<}\tau\texttt{>} \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash a : \sigma \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash f : \sigma \times \tau \to \sigma}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash \texttt{fold\_left(}r\texttt{, }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} : \sigma}
\qquad
\dfrac{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash x : \tau \qquad \Delta; \Gamma \vdash n : \texttt{size\_t}}{\Delta; \Gamma \vdash \texttt{repeat(}x\texttt{, }n\texttt{)} : \texttt{range<}\tau\texttt{>}}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(D-Generic)}\quad \dfrac{\overline{\alpha}, \overline{Q};\; \overline{x : \tau_x} \vdash e : \tau}{\vdash \texttt{template <typename }\overline{\alpha}\texttt{> requires } \overline{Q}\texttt{ constexpr } \tau\; f\texttt{(}\overline{\tau_x\; x}\texttt{) \{ return } e\texttt{; \}}}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(D-Instance)}\quad \dfrac{\varnothing;\; \overline{x : \tau_x} \vdash e : \tau}{\vdash \texttt{template <> constexpr } \tau\; f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\overline{\tau_x\; x}\texttt{) \{ return } e\texttt{; \}}}
\end{array}
\]
\normalsize

\textsc{E-Law} is where a law is a value: entailment evaluates it, so a shape with a law is satisfied only at instances whose certificate holds, and the stratification of laws (closed terms of base type) keeps typing and evaluation from chasing each other.
\textsc{E-Op} reads $S$ as equality for \cppinline{std::same_as} and as conversion for \cppinline{std::convertible_to}.
\textsc{T-Trait-Assumed} is the rule that makes \textsc{D-Generic} a definition check: inside a constrained body a trait is reachable only through a constraint in $\Delta$.
\cppinline{iota(0, n)} is typed like \cppinline{repeat} with $\tau = $ \cppinline{size_t}.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{Evaluation}
\label{app:fjlt:evaluation}

Evaluation is big-step, $e \Downarrow v$, on closed terms, with values $v ::= c \mid f \mid O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{\}} \mid \texttt{std::pair\{}v\texttt{, }v\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::optional\{}v\texttt{\}} \mid \texttt{std::nullopt}$.
It is \Cpp{}'s constant evaluation restricted to the grammar, and Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval} gives the three rules that are not generic.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\footnotesize
\[
\begin{array}{c}
\textsc{(V-Call)}\quad \dfrac{f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>} \text{ resolves to } \ldots f\texttt{(}\overline{\tau_x\; x}\texttt{) \{ return } e\texttt{; \}} \qquad \overline{e_i \Downarrow v_i} \qquad e[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}][\overline{v}/\overline{x}] \Downarrow v}{f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \Downarrow v}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(V-Op)}\quad \dfrac{\overline{e_i \Downarrow v_i} \qquad O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \text{ applied to } \overline{v} \text{ is } v \text{ by the standard}}{O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{\}(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \Downarrow v}
\\[12pt]
\textsc{(V-Fold)}\quad \dfrac{x \Downarrow v_x \qquad n \Downarrow k \qquad a \Downarrow s_0 \qquad f\texttt{(}s_{i-1}\texttt{, }v_x\texttt{)} \Downarrow s_i \text{ for } i = 1 \ldots k}{\texttt{fold\_left(repeat(}x\texttt{, }n\texttt{), }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} \Downarrow s_k}
\end{array}
\]
\normalsize
\caption{Evaluation.
\textsc{V-Call} covers specialisations and generic functions alike, since resolution has already chosen the body; for a generic function the explicit type arguments are substituted first.
\textsc{V-Fold} over \cppinline{iota(0, n)} feeds $i - 1$ in place of $v_x$ at step $i$.
Constants, pairs, optionals and conditionals evaluate as expected, and the arithmetic of base types is the standard's, which at compile time refuses undefined behaviour.}
\label{fig:fjlt-eval}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Translation into System F}
\label{app:fjlt:translation}

The target is System~F with products, sums and the base types of Section~\ref{sec:posture} taken as constants with their operations; records are nested pairs, and the base types could be Church-encoded, so the target is conservative over System~F.
Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-translation} gives the translation.
Its one non-local ingredient is the \emph{dictionary} of an entailment: $\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket$ is the record that witnesses $Q$, built by the same recursion as resolution.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\footnotesize
\[
\begin{array}{l@{\quad}l}
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{types}} \\
\llbracket \alpha \rrbracket = \alpha \qquad \llbracket b \rrbracket = b \qquad \llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket = 1 & \text{a function-object type has one value} \\
\llbracket \texttt{std::pair<}\tau_1\texttt{, }\tau_2\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \llbracket \tau_1 \rrbracket \times \llbracket \tau_2 \rrbracket \qquad \llbracket \texttt{std::optional<}\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket = 1 + \llbracket \tau \rrbracket & \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{constraints, as record types}} \\
\llbracket C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \{\,\overline{r_i : \llbracket R_i \rrbracket[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}]}\,\} \text{ where } \texttt{concept } C \texttt{ = } \overline{R} & \text{one field per requirement} \\
\llbracket Q \rrbracket \text{ for a nested shape } Q & \text{its record type} \\
\llbracket e \rrbracket = \mathit{Bool} \text{ for a law } e & \\
\llbracket \texttt{\{ }e\texttt{ \} -> }S\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \overline{\llbracket \tau_x \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket & \text{an operation on parameters } \overline{x : \tau_x} \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{dictionaries, from entailment derivations}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q \rrbracket = q \text{ if } Q \in \Delta \text{ with record variable } q & \text{\textsc{E-Assume}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash C\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \{\,\overline{r_i = \llbracket \Delta \Vdash R_i[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}\,\} & \text{\textsc{E-Shape}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash e \rrbracket = \mathit{true} & \text{\textsc{E-Law}} \\
\llbracket \Delta \Vdash \texttt{\{ }e\texttt{ \} -> }S\texttt{<}\tau\texttt{>} \rrbracket = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{\textsc{E-Op}} \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{declarations}} \\
\texttt{template <> constexpr } \tau\; f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\overline{x}\texttt{) \{ return } e\texttt{; \}} \;\mapsto\; f_{\overline{\tau}} = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{an instance} \\
\texttt{template <}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{> requires } \overline{Q}\texttt{ ... } f\texttt{(}\overline{x}\texttt{) \{ return } e\texttt{; \}} \;\mapsto\; f = \Lambda\overline{\alpha}.\,\lambda\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}.\,\lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{a generic function} \\
\texttt{struct } O \texttt{ \{ ... operator()(}\overline{x}\texttt{) ... \{ return } e\texttt{; \} \};} \;\mapsto\; O = \lambda\overline{x}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket & \text{an operation} \\
\texttt{concept}, \texttt{= delete} \;\mapsto\; \text{nothing} & \\[6pt]
\multicolumn{2}{l}{\textit{terms}} \\
\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = f\,\overline{\llbracket \sigma \rrbracket}\,\overline{\llbracket \Delta \Vdash Q[\overline{\sigma}/\overline{\alpha}] \rrbracket}\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Call}} \\
\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\sigma}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = f_{\overline{\sigma}}\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Trait}, resolved to a specialisation} \\
\llbracket f\texttt{<}\overline{\alpha}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = q.f\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Trait-Assumed}} \\
\llbracket O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{\}(}\overline{e}\texttt{)} \rrbracket = O\,\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket} & \text{\textsc{T-Op}} \\
\llbracket \texttt{fold\_left(repeat(}x\texttt{, }n\texttt{), }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} \rrbracket = \llbracket n \rrbracket\,[\sigma]\,(\lambda s.\,\llbracket f \rrbracket\,s\,\llbracket x \rrbracket)\,\llbracket a \rrbracket & \text{the numeral} \\
\llbracket \texttt{fold\_left(iota(0, }n\texttt{), }a\texttt{, }f\texttt{)} \rrbracket = \pi_2\,(\llbracket n \rrbracket\,[\mathbb{N} \times \sigma]\,\mathit{step}\,(0, \llbracket a \rrbracket)) & \text{primitive recursion} \\
\qquad \text{where } \mathit{step} = \lambda p.\,(\pi_1 p + 1,\; \llbracket f \rrbracket\,(\pi_2 p)\,(\pi_1 p)) & \\
\llbracket e_1 \texttt{ ? } e_2 \texttt{ : } e_3 \rrbracket = \mathbf{if}\ \llbracket e_1 \rrbracket\ \mathbf{then}\ \llbracket e_2 \rrbracket\ \mathbf{else}\ \llbracket e_3 \rrbracket & \text{case on } \mathit{Bool} \\
\llbracket \texttt{std::optional\{}e\texttt{\}} \rrbracket = \mathrm{inr}\,\llbracket e \rrbracket \qquad \llbracket \texttt{std::nullopt} \rrbracket = \mathrm{inl}\,() & \\
\end{array}
\]
\normalsize
\caption{The translation.
A trait has no image of its own; its specialisations are closed terms $f_{\overline{\tau}}$, and a generic function that serves as an instance with a context is the function from records to a record that \textsc{E-Shape} builds its dictionary with.
Where \cppinline{size_t} is read as $\mathbb{N}$, $\llbracket n \rrbracket$ is the Church numeral of the value of $n$; the translation of a term with a runtime \cppinline{n} is the term with $n$ as a free variable of numeral type, and Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation} is stated for closed terms.}
\label{fig:fjlt-translation}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Theorems}
\label{app:fjlt:theorems}
% Proof sketches for the statements of Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}.  They
% rest on the typing rules (A.2), the evaluation rules (A.3) and the
% translation (A.4), which are still to be written; each sketch names the
% cases it needs from them.  Full proofs and a mechanisation are future work.

The statements are in Section~\ref{sec:guarantees}; here are the sketches.
Throughout, the translation $\llbracket \cdot \rrbracket$ is the one of Section~\ref{app:fjlt:translation}, into System~F with products and records, which is conservative over System~F proper (records are nested pairs, pairs are Church-encoded).

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, type preservation]
By induction on the typing derivation of $\Delta; \Gamma \vdash e : \tau$, with one case per rule of Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing}.
Three cases carry the weight.
\emph{Generic function.}
A declaration $\texttt{template <typename }\overline{\alpha}\texttt{> requires }\overline{Q}\texttt{ constexpr }\tau\; f(\overline{\tau\; x})\ \{\ \texttt{return } e;\ \}$ is typed with $\overline{\alpha}$ abstract and the constraints $\overline{Q}$ as assumptions on them.
Its translation is $\Lambda\overline{\alpha}.\,\lambda\overline{q : \llbracket Q \rrbracket}.\,\lambda\overline{x : \llbracket \tau \rrbracket}.\,\llbracket e \rrbracket$, and the induction hypothesis for $e$ holds in the context extended by the record variables $\overline{q}$, since every operation $e$ uses on an $\alpha$ is, by the typing rule, a field of one of the $\overline{Q}$; this is where the rule that bodies are typed against their constraints does its work.
\emph{Call.}
At $f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)}$ the typing rule demands that each instantiated constraint $Q[\overline{\tau}/\overline{\alpha}]$ be \emph{entailed}, i.e. resolved to an instance; the translation applies $\llbracket f \rrbracket$ to $\overline{\llbracket \tau \rrbracket}$, then to the records those instances translate to, then to $\overline{\llbracket e \rrbracket}$.
Each record has the demanded type by the \emph{instance} case, so the application is well-typed.
\emph{Instance.}
An explicit specialisation of a trait at concrete types translates to a record value whose field types are the trait's return type at those types; an instance with a context (a constrained generic function) translates, by the first case, to a function from the context's records to such a record.
The remaining cases (variables, constants, function-object application, pairs, laws as \texttt{Bool} fields) are immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation}, simulation]
By induction on the evaluation derivation $e \Downarrow v$ of Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval}, showing $\llbracket e \rrbracket \longrightarrow^{*} \llbracket v \rrbracket$.
\emph{Call.}
$f\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\overline{e}\texttt{)}$ evaluates by resolving the instances, evaluating the arguments, and evaluating the body with the resolved specialisations in scope.
On the System~F side, $\llbracket f \rrbracket\,\overline{\llbracket \tau \rrbracket}\,\overline{d}\,\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$ $\beta$-reduces to $\llbracket e_f \rrbracket$ with the records $\overline{d}$ substituted for $\overline{q}$; a trait read $t\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>(}\ldots\texttt{)}$ in the body has become a field projection $q.t$, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence} the field holds the translation of the specialisation FJlt resolved.
\emph{Function objects.}
$O\texttt{<}\overline{\tau}\texttt{>\{\}(}\overline{v}\texttt{)}$ evaluates by the standard's definition of $O$; its translation is the closure $\llbracket O \rrbracket$ applied to $\overline{\llbracket v \rrbracket}$, and the closure is chosen so that the two agree on constants.
\emph{Fuel.}
\cppinline{fold_left(repeat(x, n), a, f)} evaluates by applying $f$ to the accumulator and $x$, $n$ times; it translates to the Church numeral $\overline{n}$ applied to $\lambda a.\,\llbracket f \rrbracket\,a\,\llbracket x \rrbracket$ and $\llbracket a \rrbracket$, and $n$ steps are $n$ unfoldings of the numeral.
\cppinline{fold_left(iota(0, n), a, f)} is primitive recursion: the numeral applied to a step on pairs $(i, a) \mapsto (i + 1, f\,a\,i)$, projected.
For the converse at base types: a closed System~F term of type $\llbracket b \rrbracket$ normalises to a constant (strong normalisation plus the shape of normal forms at base types), both evaluations are deterministic, and the forward direction already identifies that constant with $\llbracket v \rrbracket$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:coherence}, coherence]
Resolution is defined in Section~\ref{app:fjlt:typing} as a relation between a trait at concrete types and the declarations that match it; the claim is that this relation is a partial function, and well-formedness demands it.
For an explicit specialisation, two declarations at the same types are a redefinition, which the well-formedness conditions on programs forbid (in \Cpp{}, the one-definition rule).
For instances with contexts, the design decision of no overlapping instances means that at most one constrained generic function matches a given constraint at concrete types; in \Cpp{}, two that match are an ambiguous call, which is ill-formed, and one that matches only under its context resolves the context first, recursively, and the recursion is well-founded because a context mentions only proper parts of the types the instance is for.
So a well-formed program resolves each constraint at each concrete instantiation to at most one record, and the translation, which is defined by that resolution, is a function of the program.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Corollary~\ref{cor:termination}, termination]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation}, an FJlt evaluation of $e$ is simulated by a reduction of $\llbracket e \rrbracket$, a System~F term with products; System~F with products is strongly normalising (Girard; see Girard, Lafont and Taylor, \emph{Proofs and Types}), so the reduction, and with it the evaluation, is finite.
Fuel enters as a Church numeral, i.e. as a term, not as a recursion scheme the type system would have to admit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Corollary~\ref{cor:parametricity}, parametricity]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, a generic FJlt function translates to a closed System~F term of type $\forall\overline{\alpha}.\,\overline{\llbracket Q \rrbracket} \to \overline{\llbracket \tau \rrbracket} \to \llbracket \tau \rrbracket$; Reynolds' abstraction theorem gives its free theorem, and the record parameters are ordinary term parameters in it, which is why the theorem relates behaviour at two instantiations through the relation between their records.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of Corollary~\ref{cor:soundness}, type soundness]
Let $e$ be closed and well-typed at a base type $b$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:preservation}, $\llbracket e \rrbracket$ is a closed System~F term of type $\llbracket b \rrbracket$; by strong normalisation it has a normal form, and a closed normal form at $\llbracket b \rrbracket$ is a constant $c$ (no variable is free, no $\lambda$ or $\Lambda$ has that type, and the eliminators of the encoded base types reduce on constants).
The converse half of Theorem~\ref{thm:simulation} then gives $e \Downarrow v$ with $\llbracket v \rrbracket = c$.
Nothing here is specific to FJlt beyond the two theorems; it is the standard argument that a translation into a sound calculus is sound.
\end{proof}

What the sketches do not do: they induct over the rules of Figures~\ref{fig:fjlt-typing} and~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval} as given, which omit the generic cases, and they take \cppinline{fold_left} over \cppinline{repeat} and \cppinline{iota} as primitives with the evaluation rule of Figure~\ref{fig:fjlt-eval}, trusting the standard library for it as Section~\ref{sec:translation:adequacy} says.
Full proofs, and a mechanisation, are future work.
