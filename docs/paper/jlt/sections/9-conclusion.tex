\subsection{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Three rules on standard \Cpp{23} were stated, drawn from four listings: a signature that prevents a bug, a law that permits a faster program, a dependency that a signature must declare, and laws the compiler derives at a composite.
A program that keeps them has an image in System~F by dictionary passing, the translation that gives Haskell's type classes their meaning, and the image is parametric, sound and terminating.
That the image computes what \Cpp{} computes is the paper's hypothesis of adequacy, stated and not proved (Section~\ref{sec:translation}).

The compiler holds no rule in full: Rule~2, and Rules~1 and~3 outside compile-time evaluation, are held by the author, a linter and an agentic reviewer (Table~\ref{tab:posture}, Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

Four things follow as work not done here.
Widening the linter's second stage, which admits a minority of the library's definitions today, and testing its conjecture on code not written under the rules.
Certifying that a run-time shortcut agrees with the fold it replaces, which a branch on the binding time leaves to a law or a reader: the third homomorphism theorem characterises when a fold can be split~\cite{gibbons1996third}, and a shape could declare it.
A mechanisation of the proofs, the aspirational end, as Siek and Lumsdaine did for $F^G$ in Isabelle~\cite{siek2004formalization}.
And laws that are computed with rather than only declared, which needs terms and their composition.

Declared laws, and the laws derived from them, license optimisations of the application's own algebra, as types rule out errors: by a sound check, not a decision of equivalence.
The same laws are those a query optimiser applies to relational algebra~\cite[Chapter~6]{abiteboul1995foundations}.
Nothing allocated during constant evaluation outlives it, so the persistent structures of functional programming, built on the heap, do not cross to run time.
What crosses is state whose size is fixed at compile time, and the computations that specialise well across it may be those that run in small state over an unbounded input, the streaming algorithms~\cite{alon1999space}.
Nothing was added to the language, the compiler or the library: what was added is a boundary, inside which a mainstream compiler checks claims of a modest but real kind about programs that then run unchanged, for the people who already write \Cpp{23}.
The boundary is meant as a route rather than a wall: a team takes it one file and one rule at a time, the linter says at each step where the code stands, and what it cannot settle it names.
