\begin{abstract}
Parametric polymorphism, type classes and structural recursion are the tools with which a functional programmer says what a program does and has a type checker hold it to that.
\Cpp{23}, which runs much of machine learning and most embedded devices, has the material for them: compile-time evaluation of ordinary functions and of most of the standard library, concepts, and folds over ranges.
It does not, however, hold a generic function to its declared constraints: the body may use any operation its type argument happens to have.

This paper characterises a fragment of \Cpp{23} as it ships, with no language modification, compiler extension or tool to install: an embedded language whose domain is System~F and whose programs are ordinary \Cpp{23} programs.
The fragment is type-class-style code under three rules, which we call the Juliet Posture (Jlt):
i) values are copyable data and functions are pure and evaluable at compile time;
ii) generic code varies with a type only through that type's concepts and traits;
iii) recursion is structural, on a type or on a natural number that counts down.
Its kernel, Featherweight Jlt (FJlt), translates into System~F by dictionary passing, as Haskell's type classes do, and the translation is total and preserves types and evaluation, so an FJlt program inherits termination, parametricity and a value.
What Jlt writes beyond the kernel is sugar, named with its desugaring, and that the desugaring keeps meaning is the paper's working hypothesis.
Declared laws license overloads, and overload resolution composes them: in one program the action law and associativity, neither aware of the other, turn a fold of $n$ steps into one of $\log n$, even at compile time, where no optimiser runs.
In another, the words sought share a symmetry, said in their type.
An automaton would list them, exponentially in their length; the test the law writes is linear in it, and the compiler vectorises it.
Along the way the paper shows that \Cpp{20} concepts with overlapping overloads resolve incoherently in GHC's sense, and adopts GHC's rule.
The evidence is proofs relative to stated assumptions, listings that compile and run at both binding times under a test in the public repository, their compiled output, and a library whose core a linter checks against the rules.

The claim is one of existence: \Cpp{23} already contains an embedding of a fragment of System~F, held by the compiler where the standard checks and by the programmer's discipline elsewhere, and available today wherever a stock \Cpp{23} compiler is.
\end{abstract}
